Bizzing Bee — Privacy Policy and Notice to Parents

Effective 30 August 2026 · Written for parents and guardians, under the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA, 16 CFR Part 312)

The short version

We do not hold a record of your child. Not their name, not their age, not a photo, a voice recording or a location. What can leave the device is a display name, a spelling level and their practice progress, stored against a random id. The database has no column for a real name or an age, so neither can be collected later by accident.

The app works completely offline, and that is still the default. Everything is saved on your device first. Nothing is sent anywhere until you, the parent, create an account and switch cloud backup on.

If you do switch it on, one piece of personal information is held: your email address, because somebody has to own the account. It is an adult's, given by an adult. There is no analytics, no advertising, no trackers, and nobody's information is sold or shared.

Your child never signs in to anything, and is never asked for an email address, a phone number, a photo or a location. Sections 3 and 9 explain exactly what travels and what does not.

Bizzing Bee is a spelling-bee training app for children aged roughly 8–15, operated by its developer ("we"). Questions about this policy or your child's information:

2. Two modes, and you choose

Bizzing Bee runs in one of two modes, and it starts in the first one:

  • On-device (the default). No account, no sign-in, nothing transmitted. Profiles and progress live in your browser's own storage. The app is fully playable this way, forever, and nothing in it is withheld from you for staying here.
  • With a parent account and cloud backup (optional). You create an account with your email address and turn backup on. From then on each speller's display name, spelling level and progress are copied to our database so a wiped, lost or replaced tablet does not cost your child months of work, and so the same progress appears on a second device.

Switching from the first to the second is a deliberate act by an adult, taken behind the parent PIN, on a screen that says plainly what will be sent. You can switch back at any time (section 7).

3. What information the app handles — and where it lives

When you create a speller profile, the app asks for a first name (or nickname) and an age, and lets your child pick an avatar. As your child plays it records practice progress: words attempted, scores, levels, coins and settings. All of that is stored on your device.

The name and the age never leave the device — not the real name, and not a nickname either. They are used only to greet your child on screen and to pick a starting difficulty. When backup is on, what is copied to the server is a label taken from the avatar your child picked ("Fox", "Panda"), their spelling level, and the progress record — against a random id that is not derived from anything about them. When you restore onto a new device, the app asks you for the name and the age there, because the server was never told them.

4. Verifiable parental consent

COPPA requires an operator to obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information online from a child under 13. Bizzing Bee is built so that the question mostly does not arise — the app collects no personal information from children at all, and what it backs up is pseudonymous by design. Even so, because backup means data about a child leaves the device, we treat turning it on as a consent event and handle it as follows:

  • Backup can only be switched on from the Parent Zone, behind the parent PIN, by someone who has created an account with a working email address and confirmed it by clicking a link we send there. A child cannot reach this screen or complete this step.
  • The consent screen states, in plain language and before anything is sent, exactly what will be uploaded, what will not, who stores it, and how to withdraw.
  • We record the date and time consent was given against the account, so it can be shown back to you and audited.
  • Consent can be withdrawn at any time, in one tap, from the same screen. Withdrawing it stops all further uploads immediately and deletes what was already uploaded (section 7).

If a future version of the app collects anything genuinely identifying from a child, this policy will be updated before that ships, the effective date above will move, and we will give direct notice and obtain consent by one of the methods the FTC recognises before any such collection begins.

5. Who else touches this information

Two service providers, and no one else. Neither is permitted to use the information for their own purposes; both act only on our instructions.

  • Supabase (supabase.com/privacy) hosts the database and the sign-in service. This is where the parent's email address, the hashed password, and — if backup is on — the pseudonymous speller records are stored. Rows are protected by row-level security, which means a signed-in account can reach only its own family's rows and nothing else, enforced by the database itself rather than by the app asking nicely.
  • GitHub serves the pages, artwork and audio (section 6).

There is no advertising network, no analytics provider, no crash reporter, no marketing tool and no social platform in this app. Nothing is sold. Nothing is shared for anyone else's benefit.

Optional research capture (off by default). For families who help us play-test, Settings → Testing tools has a PIN-gated switch called Research capture. While it is on, the app keeps a technical log on that device only: which screens were open, where taps landed, timings, software errors, and an optional end-of-session smiley. The log never contains a name, an age, or anything a child typed, and the app never sends it anywhere — a parent can review, export (as a file to share with us themselves), or erase it at any time from the same switch. Turning the switch off stops the log immediately. The same is true of the in-app "Report a bug" panel: reports stay on the device until a parent exports them.

6. Web hosting and IP addresses

Like every website, the pages, artwork and audio of Bizzing Bee are delivered by a web host — GitHub Pages, with word-audio files served from GitHub's content servers. To deliver a file, a web host necessarily receives the requesting device's IP address, and may keep it briefly in standard server logs for security and traffic purposes; the same is true of the database host when backup is on. COPPA treats an IP address as a persistent identifier; it is used here solely to support the internal operations of the service (delivering content, storing the backup you asked for, and maintaining security), which COPPA permits without parental consent. It is not used to profile, track, advertise to or contact anyone. GitHub's handling of server logs is described in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

7. Your rights as a parent

You can exercise every COPPA right from inside the app, immediately, without asking us and without waiting for a reply:

  • Review — open the Parent Zone (Progress → Parent zone, behind your parent PIN) to see everything the app knows: profiles, progress, scores and settings. What is backed up is a subset of what you see there, described in section 3.
  • Delete a speller — remove the profile in Settings. The local copy goes, and if backup is on, the row and its progress are deleted from the database with it.
  • Withdraw consent / turn backup off — one tap in the Parent Zone. Uploads stop at once and everything already uploaded for your account is deleted. The app keeps working on the device exactly as before, with nothing leaving it.
  • Delete the whole account — deleting the parent account deletes every speller row and every progress record attached to it, permanently and by database cascade, along with the email address. If you would rather we did it, email us and we will, and confirm when it is done.
  • Refuse further collection while keeping the app — sign out, or simply never create an account. Neither costs your child a single feature.

8. How long information is kept

Information on your device is kept for as long as you keep it: until you delete the profile or clear the browser's storage for this app. Information in the backup is kept for as long as the account exists and backup is on — delete the account, or withdraw consent, and it is deleted. We keep no separate copy and no archive, and there is nothing identifying a child in it to keep. Standard server logs (section 6) age out on our hosts' own schedules.

9. Payments

The app and its opening page describe optional paid plans and show their prices, but no payment is collected inside the app today, no card details are ever requested, and no payment processor is integrated. Choosing a plan today records a preference on your account and charges you nothing.

When payments are switched on, purchases will be a parent-only flow: behind the parent PIN, with any personal or payment information collected from the parent — never the child — and handled by a named payment processor which we will identify here. Card numbers will go to that processor and not to us. This policy and the notices in the app will be updated before the first payment is taken, and the effective date above will move.

10. Security

The best protection for children's data is not to collect it, and that is still the design here: the two things that would identify a child — their real name and their age — have nowhere to go, because the database has no column for either.

For what does travel:

  • Every request is over HTTPS. Nothing is sent in the clear.
  • Your password is hashed by the authentication service and never stored, logged or seen by us in readable form. Sign-in is checked on the server, not in your browser.
  • Row-level security is the access model, not a convention: each row records which account owns it, and the database refuses to return a row to anyone else. The public key shipped in the app identifies the project and grants no access on its own.
  • The parent PIN keeps children out of Settings, the Parent Zone, account screens and anything else marked for grown-ups. It is a local gate on your device, not a password — someone holding your unlocked device can still see what is on it.

Please choose a password you do not use anywhere else. That is ordinary good practice for any account, and it costs nothing to follow.

Earlier versions of Bizzing Bee stored a parent account in the browser with only a scrambled password, and this policy warned about it. That local-only account is gone; accounts are now checked and hashed on a server, which is the improvement that warning promised.

11. Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices change in a material way, this policy will be updated before the change ships, the effective date above will move, and where COPPA requires it we will provide direct notice and obtain verifiable parental consent before any new collection begins.

12. About Bizzing Bee & sources

Bizzing Bee is an independent study tool. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by the Scripps National Spelling Bee, the North South Foundation, or Merriam-Webster. Those names appear in the app only to describe which competitions the practice material relates to — for example, a list of words that have appeared in past bees.

Definitions, example sentences and hints are written for this app and are not taken from any official dictionary. At a real bee, the official dictionary — Merriam-Webster Unabridged, for Scripps — is the final authority on spellings and meanings.

Word pronunciations are synthesized audio. Typefaces are open-licensed (SIL OFL); lesson narration uses the Apache-licensed Kokoro voice model. Third-party licences are listed in the project's LICENSES-THIRD-PARTY.md.
