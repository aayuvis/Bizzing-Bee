Bizzing Bee · Brand platform · App + Books

Two audiences.
One product.
Do not confuse them.

Bizzing Bee is not a generic kids' spelling app that happens to have hard words in it. It is built on NSF Finals and Scripps-level data, with a parent coaching engine whose categories are bee day, nerves, plateau and oral rounds. That is not homework help. That is competition preparation.

Which means the campaign has two speeds, and running one message at both audiences would waste the product's single biggest advantage: it is genuinely serious, in a category that is genuinely not.

Win the circuit on credibility.
Win the country on fame.

01 · The audience, before anything else

There is a core, and there is a country.

The product tells you who it was built for. The word data is North South Foundation Finals and Scripps. The vocabulary set is tagged NSF 2026 Junior. The parent tips include how to breathe backstage and what to do in the fortnight before a bee. Nobody builds that for a child who just wants to pass Friday's test.

Ring 1 · The beachhead

The competitive bee family

Children 8–14 on the NSF and Scripps circuit. In the US this community is concentrated in South Asian diaspora families — highly organised, chapter-based, and already spending real money on coaches, workbooks and word lists. They do not need persuading that spelling matters. They need to believe your word coverage, pronunciation accuracy and bee-day readiness beat what they use now.

Ring 2 · The volume

The ambitious mainstream parent

Would never enter a bee. Wants their child ahead, and wants proof. Buys on visible progress and on what other parents in the class group say. This is where the fame campaign earns its money.

Ring 3 · The anxious parent

The child who is behind

Spelling has become a fight. They want remediation without tears. The story mode and the arcade matter more to them than the ladder — the product's job is that the child doesn't notice it's work.

Ring 4 · The multiplier

Coaches, tutors and schools

Small in number, disproportionate in influence. One bee coach recommending this reaches thirty families who are already paying for help. The cheapest distribution in the entire plan.

What the core actually worries about

Not "is it fun". The product's own coaching engine is organised around sixteen parent problems, and they are the real brief:

Re-engaging after a breakConsistency Hard wordsMemory AccuracyOral rounds Written roundsBee day NervesMotivation PlateauReview CoverageWord origins

Two of those — bee day and nerves — only exist for a family with a competition in the calendar. That is the clearest evidence of who this was built for.

Why the order matters commercially

The beachhead is a community, not a media buy. It is reachable through NSF regional chapters, coaches, and parent groups where a recommendation travels faster than any ad. It is small, but it converts high, pays annually, and stays for years.

Ring 2 is a hundred times larger and almost impossible to convert cold — unless it arrives pre-endorsed. Winning the circuit first is what makes the mainstream campaign credible rather than just another app ad.

Sequence: earn the core, then buy the country. Spending fame money before the circuit vouches for you wastes the one thing competitors cannot copy.
Two things to verify before this is presented as fact: the real split of current users between competitive and mainstream families, and whether the Australian and UK markets have an equivalent circuit — the NSF structure is specifically American, and the strategy does not port without a local equivalent.

02 · What that means for the work

Nobody abandons a game they're winning.

The edtech graveyard is full of beautiful apps that got downloaded once. The category talks to parents about learning outcomes and never once solves the thing that actually kills these products: the child stops opening it.

So the campaign should not argue that Bizzing Bee is educational. Parents assume that, and every competitor claims it. It should argue that the child keeps going — and prove it with the only evidence a parent trusts, which is their own child doing something they couldn't do last month.

The strategic filter, applied to every idea here: show the child ahead of the adult. If an execution could run for a generic "fun learning" app, it isn't a Bizzing Bee idea. Progress you can see beats progress you're promised.

06 · The work — ring 1, the circuit

For the core, credibility is the creative.

This audience does not want to be charmed. They want to know your word list is deeper, your pronunciation is right, and your plan holds in the fortnight before a bee. Same brand, no jokes — the register here is a serious tool talking to serious people.

Every one of these comes straight out of the product's own coaching data, which means the brand is saying things no competitor can credibly say.

Distribution for this tier is not media. It is NSF regional chapters, coaches, tutoring networks and parent groups — plus a free, genuinely useful artefact (the bee-day plan, the printable word list) that circulates on its own.

07 · The work — books

Where it starts.

The app is for the child who wants to win. The books are for the child who doesn't know they like words yet — and for the parent who wants a bedtime story, not a screen. Fifteen packs, one hero, a cast that turns vocabulary into characters.

08 · How the two products work together

One ladder, ages 4 to 15.

This is the commercial argument for running them as one brand rather than two. The books recruit; the app retains; each makes the other worth more.

Ages 4–7

The books

Bizzy and the crew. Read aloud by a parent. No screen, no scoring, no pressure. The job is affection for words — and for a character the child will follow.

Ages 7–9

The story mode

The same characters, now playable. Six acts, thirty-one chapters. The child thinks they are playing a game about a bee. They are learning to spell.

Ages 9–12

The ladder

Levels 1–20, the ~1,600 highest-value words, an avatar that evolves the whole way up. Champ Challenges to test out and skip ahead.

Ages 12–15

The library

Levels 21+ and competitive bee preparation. The child who started with a picture book is now working through words most adults cannot spell.

The ladder is also the retention story a subscription business needs: a family that enters at four has eleven years of reasons to stay. Price and package the books as the top of the funnel, not as merchandise.

09 · Channel plan

Two buyers, one child

Buyer
Parents 32–50 of children 4–15. They hold the card and the guilt. They are not looking for another app; they are looking for evidence they made a good call.
User
The child. They decide whether it survives past week three. Nothing in the campaign should bore them, and the story world is what they actually respond to.
Segments
The competitive family aiming at a bee · the anxious family whose child is behind · the bookish family who want less screen, not more · the homeschool and tutoring segment.
Channels
Meta and TikTok for the out-spelled films (they are natively shareable), YouTube pre-roll, parenting newsletters and podcasts, school and tutor partnerships, and the single highest-value channel: other parents in the same class group.
Timing
Back-to-school is the obvious spike; the sharper opportunity is the first spelling-test week of term, when the pain is specific and immediate.

10 · How it gets judged

Retention, not installs

Primary
Day-30 and day-90 active rate. The whole strategy rests on the child still opening it. If this number moves, the campaign worked.
Primary
Words mastered per active child per week — the number that produces the dinner-table moment the advertising promises.
Secondary
Trial-to-paid conversion; books-to-app crossover rate, which is the whole argument for one brand.
Fame
Branded search for "Bizzing Bee", and share rate on the out-spelled films — this format lives or dies on being sent between parents.
Not a KPI
Installs alone. An install with no week-three open is a cost, not a win.
The riskiest assumption in this platform is that the product retains. Before spending on fame, instrument retention properly and read it honestly — if children do drop at week three, fix that first. Advertising a leaky product just fills the bucket faster.
