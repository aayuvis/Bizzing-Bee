⬡ a memory like a goldfish
forgetting things almost instantly
From the belief that goldfish remember only three seconds — a myth; they can actually remember for months.
⬡ a mind like a steel trap
a mind that grasps and holds ideas instantly
American, 1800s — a steel trap snaps shut fast and never lets go.
⬡ a miss is as good as a mile
a narrow failure is still a failure
English, 1600s; originally 'an inch in a miss is as good as an ell'.
⬡ a voice like a foghorn
an extremely loud, deep voice
Foghorns had to out-shout the sea to warn ships lost in fog.
⬡ as agile as a monkey
quick and nimble in every movement
Monkeys swing branch to branch with effortless ease; a staple of English simile lists.
⬡ as American as apple pie
thoroughly typical of American life and values
American, early 1900s; apple pie was already the symbol of home cooking in the United States.
⬡ as bald as a coot
completely bald
The coot, a water bird, has a bare white patch on its forehead; recorded since the 1400s.
⬡ as bald as an eagle
completely bald
American; the bald eagle isn't bald — 'balde' meant white-headed in old English.
⬡ as big as a house
very large
Plain hyperbole — a house is the biggest thing on an ordinary street; common since the 1800s.
⬡ as big as an elephant
enormous
The elephant is the largest land animal, the classic classroom yardstick for hugeness.
⬡ as bitter as gall
extremely bitter, in taste or in feeling
Gall is bile, the most bitter fluid the body makes; the comparison runs through the Bible and medieval medicine.
⬡ as bitter as wormwood
intensely bitter
Wormwood is a famously bitter herb; the Book of Proverbs uses it for bitterness itself.
⬡ as black as a raven
glossy deep black, especially hair
From the Bible's Song of Solomon, where the beloved's hair is 'black as a raven'.
⬡ as black as coal
deep black
Coal was every hearth's black standard before electricity.
