02Page, grid, budgets
The rules that never move
Trim 8.5 × 11 in. Bleed 0.125 in. Safe 0.5 in, binding side 0.75 in. Twelve columns, 0.25 in gutters, 14 pt baseline. Every template declares a content budget, because the generator's fit audit rejects any page that grows past 10 in of content.
binding 0.75 in · safe 0.5 in · 12 cols
Column recipes
12 — comic opener, world divider, big list, certificate. 6+6 — practice cards left / art cue right. 8+4 — practice bench with guide cameo rail. 4+4+4 — word-family sorting, three-step worked example.
Whitespace law
One clear task per page and ≥ 25% empty paper (general band). Write-lines 0.4 in general, 0.32 in advanced, 100% ink at 1 pt — never screened, never grey. A cramped line teaches a kid they got it wrong before they start.
Never
More than two task types on one page. A word table opening a page. Text on art without a card plate behind it. Justified type, anywhere, ever.

04Colour & texture
The app's palette, on paper
Paper is never white — it is the app's lavender, so the page reads warm-cool like the product and never glares. Colour carries meaning, not decoration: a kid learns the code by page three.

Colour grammar
Green = right, done, mastered. Coral = tricky, careful, Vex. Sky #2E8FB8 = listen, say it out loud, audio. Gold = reward, streak, Bee Break. Accent = which volume you're holding, nothing else.
Pro Move box
Ink field, gold emphasis, white body. One per chapter — the champion's method. It is the only dark field on a practice spread.
Ink on paper
Ink on lavender = 12.4:1. Keep every body setting above 7:1. Rich black is banned for text — four-colour type on a kids' press goes fuzzy. Max two full-bleed accent fields per spread. Coral warnings always carry words too, never colour alone.
Texture families
Rings · stripes · dots · diag (plus grid, cross) — the app's cover-card families, plus honeycomb, at 6–10% opacity. Nothing photographic, nothing flat grey.

Panel grammar
Rounded panels, 14 pt radius, 2.5 pt ink outline, gutters 0.12 in. Tail always points at the speaker's mouth, never at the reader. Characters break the panel border only when excited — that is the one exception. Flat fills plus one soft shade; no gradients, no gloss.
Mood → pose
The six-scene scripts already carry moods: happy · think · oops · excited · love. Mood drives pose and expression; the scene payload becomes the in-panel prop; the spoken line compresses to a bubble. oops is the Vex beat, excited is the win.

07The chapter loop
One concept, five moves, always this order
The story is not decoration around the teaching — the concept is how the story gets won. Difficulty climbs inside a chapter, never across it. A chapter never ends on a test.
1
Meet it — comic opener
The six-scene script becomes a 3–5 panel strip. The guide acts the beats, Vex causes the trouble, the concept arrives inside the action. 1 page · 12-col
2
Understand it — Big Idea + Pro Move
One panel where the guide turns to the reader with the rule and a worked example, then the dark Pro Move box. No grey rule box, ever. ½ page
3
Practise it — Hive, then Rapid Round
Write-in cards for this concept only, then compact one-liner rows. Concept-mates study together; a Vex Alert plants one deliberate misspelling. 2–4 pages · 8+4 or 6+6
4
Prove it — mini-check, kid-marked
Ten numbered lines and a Check Yourself the child marks. Answers live in the back. Missed one routes back to its page — it never ends in a score. 1 page, deliberately plain
5
Collect it — puzzle, badge, hook
A puzzle built from this chapter's own words, the “I own this chapter” badge and sticker spot, then one comic beat that hooks the next chapter. The book gets loud again here. 2 pages
Why this order
Concept-first beats list-first: kids retain the family, not the list. So a word table never opens a page, and difficulty means trickiness — silent letters, sound-alike endings, French and Greek patterns — not rarity. Each trap carries a recurring visual motif from the spot set.
Advanced variant
Same five moves. Denser benches (16 words), deeper clue language, the worked example moves into the Pro Move so the reader infers the rule first, and the strip runs more dialogue with fewer prop tiles.

11How it sounds
The writing is the design
If a sentence sounds generated, the spread fails no matter how it looks. Coach voice: short, punchy, contractions everywhere. Headlines under eight words.
Write like this
“Vex stole a letter. Put it back.”
“Don't argue with French. Memorize it.”
“Tricky one. Let's crack it together.”
“Hear the double? Something's hiding under it.”
Never this
“Complete the following exercise by filling in the missing letters.”
“Let's dive in and unlock your spelling potential!!!”
Any mention of AI, machines, or how the book was made.
Word bank
Approved verbs: try · listen · catch · spot · build · hunt · crack · put back · beat.
Approved praise: nice catch · you found it · that's the tricky one · even Vex missed that.
Never: correct · well done, student · excellent work.
Banned words: delve · unleash · leverage · utilize · furthermore · robust · seamless · elevate · in today's world.
Sentence length
General volumes: 14 words max, one idea, read-aloud friendly. Advanced: up to 20, still active, still second person. Instructions never run past two sentences — if they do, the task is too complicated.
Who's talking
The volume guide narrates, teaches and encourages — on every spread.
Bumble asks the question the reader is already thinking, and gets stuck first.
Queen Hive gives the rule straight, in the Advanced volumes.
Waggle owns sound and syllables; Drone owns roots and word-building.
Popcorn and Melody host the collections — warmer, funnier, no drills.
Vex sneers briefly, is always wrong, is never cruel, and never gets the last laugh.
Two notations, always
Every word carries the friendly respelling and IPA, side by side, from Volume 1 — real study lists use phonetic notation, so the books normalise it early. Respelling first, both in the tile face, muted so the headword still wins.

12Access, print, ships
Three outputs, one source
Press
PDF/X-4, CMYK, 0.125 in bleed, crops and registration, 300 ppi art. Hard-offset shadows. No rich black in text.
Digital
Same layout in RGB, no bleed, legible at 100% on a 13 in screen, tagged for reading order. Soft sticker shadows allowed.
Home print
No bleed, no accent fields, mono-safe, art to line only, accents to 20% grey. Letter and A4 both fit inside the safe area.
Reachable by every reader
Leading 1.5× minimum, letter-spacing +0.01em on body, left-aligned always. Instructions never rely on colour alone — coral warnings carry words too. Write-lines at full-strength ink, 0.4 in general / 0.32 in advanced. A high-contrast B/W variant of every volume ships alongside the colour edition.
Art delivery
Layered SVG with stable ids — never flattened raster — so the generator can place, tint and scale. Declared sizes on every asset. Guides at 120 × 120 to match the app library.
Licensing, kept clean
Definitions, sentences, hints, scripts and commentary are original to this project. WordNet-derived alternates carry Princeton WordNet attribution on the credits page. No dictionary text, ever. Fonts all SIL OFL, embedded and self-hosted. Quote portraits are emblems, never likenesses. Every volume carries the non-affiliation colophon in full.
