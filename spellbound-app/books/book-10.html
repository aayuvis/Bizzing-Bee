Chapter 1 · Ego & Self — ego / auto / alterThe idea · ★★☆
The big idea
Words built on Latin ego = I and Greek autos = self describe how people relate to themselves. Master this cluster and you unlock personality vocabulary from egotist to autodidact.
The pro move
EGOTISTICAL
Say it. ee-guh-TIH-stih-kuhl Spell it. E · G · O · T · I · S · T · I · C · A · L Say it again. ee-guh-TIH-stih-kuhl Syllables. iy · gah · tih · stih · kah · l Sounds → Letters. [EE=E] [goh=GO] [TIS=TIS] [tih=TI] [kul=CAL] Exception. double-T: egotist TALKS about himself — the extra T stands for Talking; -al is the adjective suffix Origin. Latin Root. Latin ego = I + -t- + -istical = characterized by

The Core

Ego = Latin "I." An egoist cares only about themselves. An egotist constantly talks about themselves — the extra t stands for talking. Both from ego; only the suffix differs.

Scaling Up

An egocentric (ego + centrum = center) believes the universe revolves around them. An egomaniac (ego + Greek mania = madness) takes self-obsession to a pathological extreme.

The Opposite — alter

Latin alter = other. Altruistic = puts others first. Magnanimous (magna = great + animus = soul) = great-souled generosity. Philanthropist (philos = love + anthropos = human) = lover of humanity.

auto- (Greek self)

Autobiography = writes own life. Autodidact (auto- + didaskein = teach) = self-taught. Autonomous = self-governing. Autocracy = rule by one alone. When you see auto-, think "by itself/by oneself."

Vex alert
An autoNOMous region makes its own NORMS — spell NOM then add -ous, not just -us.

Chapter 2 · Talking & Speaking — loqu / phon / verbThe idea · ★★☆
The big idea
Latin loqui = to speak and Greek phonē = sound/voice are two of the most productive roots in English. Together they explain dozens of words about how, how much, and how well people communicate.
The pro move
LOQUACIOUS
Say it. l-oh-k-w-AY-sh-uh-s Spell it. L · O · Q · U · A · C · I · O · U · S Say it again. l-oh-k-w-AY-sh-uh-s Syllables. l · o · q · u · a · c · i · o · u · s Sounds → Letters. [loh=LO] [KWAY=QUA] [shus=CIOUS] Exception. qu always together; /sh/ = CIOUS (Latin adjective suffix); stress on second syllable lo-KWAY-shus Origin. Latin Root. Latin loqui = to speak

The loqu- Root

Latin loqui = to speak. Loquacious = talks too freely. Eloquent = speaks beautifully (e- = out, so speaking out well). Grandiloquent = pompously grand speech. Ventriloquist (ventri = belly) = speaks from the belly.

Silence vs Talk Scale

Taciturn (Latin tacere = silent) = habitually quiet. Reticent = reluctant to speak. Laconic (from Sparta) = brief and to-the-point. Verbose (verbum = word) = uses too many words. Garrulous = excessively chatty.

loqu- Word Families

Soliloquy (solo + loquy) = talking to yourself on stage. Colloquy (col- + loquy) = formal two-way conversation. Colloquial = everyday spoken language. Obloquy (ob- against) = harsh public criticism. Interlocutor = the person you are speaking with.

The phon- Family (Greek)

phonē = sound/voice. Always spelled ph not f — the Greek signal. Cacophony (kakos = bad) = harsh noise. Euphony (eu = good) = pleasant sound. Symphony (sym = together) = harmonious sounds. Polyphony (poly = many) = many simultaneous voices.

Chapter 3 · Honesty & Deception — ver / mendax / cred / p…The idea · ★★☆
The big idea
Latin veritas = truth and mendax = lying create perfectly opposite word families. Greek pseudos = false adds a third. These cover the full honesty spectrum and appear frequently in spelling bees.
The pro move
MENDACIOUS
Say it. m-eh-n-d-AY-sh-uh-s Spell it. M · E · N · D · A · C · I · O · U · S Say it again. m-eh-n-d-AY-sh-uh-s Syllables. m · e · n · d · a · c · i · o · u · s Sounds → Letters. [men=MEN] [DAY=DA] [shus=CIOUS] Exception. /sh/ sound = CIOUS not SHUS — the Latin adjective suffix; stress on second syllable men-DAY-shus Origin. French Root. Latin mendax = lying, from mendum = fault, defect

The Truth Root — ver-

Latin veritas = truth. Veracious = truthful (≠ voracious = greedy!). Veracity = habitual truthfulness. Verify = prove true. Verdict (ver + dict = say) = what is truly stated. Aver = firmly declare as true. Verisimilitude = the appearance of truth.

The Lying Root — mendax

Mendacious = habitually untruthful. Mendacity = the habit of lying. Memory trick: a mendacious person mends (bends) the truth. This root is rare in English — which is exactly why it keeps appearing in bees.

Deception Words

Duplicitous (duplex = double) = two-faced. Dissemble = conceal true feelings. Prevaricate = speak evasively. Equivocate (equi = equal + voc = voice) = use deliberately ambiguous language. Disingenuous = not candid, pretending to be naïve.

The cred- Family — Believing

Latin credere = to believe. Credulous = too easily believing. Incredulous = refusing to believe. Credibility = worthiness of belief. Miscreant (mis- + credere = believe wrongly) = a villain. Discredit = remove credibility.

Vex alert
VERIFY starts with VERI — VERy Important to spell it with an E, not an A!

Chapter 4 · Feelings & Emotions — the Four HumorsThe idea · ★★☆
The big idea
Medieval medicine believed four bodily fluids controlled personality. This gave English four core emotion words still used today — each with an unusual spelling pattern that spelling bees love.
The pro move
MELANCHOLY
Say it. m-EH-l-uh-n-k-ah-l-ee Spell it. M · E · L · A · N · C · H · O · L · Y Say it again. m-EH-l-uh-n-k-ah-l-ee Syllables. m · e · l · a · n · c · h · o · l · y Sounds → Letters. [MEL=MEL] [an=AN] [kol=CHOL] [ee=Y] Exception. ch = /k/ (Greek origin signal!); final -y = /ee/; do NOT spell it melancholy with double-l: mel-AN-chol-y Origin. Greek Root. Greek melas = black + khole = bile

The Four Humors

Blood → sanguine (optimistic). Yellow bile → choleric (quick-tempered). Phlegm → phlegmatic (calm, unemotional). Black bile → melancholy (deeply sad). From a 2,000-year-old medical theory — still the best set of personality adjectives in English.

Sanguine & Choleric

Sanguine (SANG-gwin) from Latin sanguis = blood; the silent e at end. Choleric (KOL-er-ik) from Greek khole = bile; ch = /k/ (Greek signal). Blood brought joy; bile brought anger. Opposite types, opposite spelling origins.

Phlegmatic — the Hardest Spelling

From Greek phlegma = mucus. Phlegm alone = P-H-L-E-G-M (silent g). Phlegmatic = P-H-L-E-G-M-A-T-I-C (silent g again). Both the ph AND the silent g are Greek signals in one word.

Positive Emotion Words

Ebullient (ebullire = boil up) = bubbling with enthusiasm. Effervescent = lively like fizzy bubbles. Exuberant = overflowing with energy. Vivacious (vivax = lively) = attractively animated. Sanguine = optimistically cheerful.

Vex alert
MELANCHOLY: MELANcholy is not about a MELON — remember black (melan) + bile (choly), not fruit.
