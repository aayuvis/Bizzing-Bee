Dutch — the largest untaught origin

The big idea
Dutch is the biggest gap in the general course: the full library holds 1,550 words of Dutch origin, more than any other language outside the classical four and French. Four centuries of shipping, trade and shared North Sea coastline pushed them in. They are recognisable — short, concrete, and clustered around the sea, the kitchen and money — and they follow a small set of spelling habits that resolve most of the choices English otherwise leaves open.
The pro move
CABOOSE
Heard. kuh-BOOSS — the last car of a train Origin. Dutch kabuis, a ship's galley Marker. Dutch writes a long oo sound as double o, not as ou or u Spell. C A B O O S E Family. schooner, sloop, cruiser — the same oo habit

Double o is the signature

Dutch writes a long oo sound as oo, and English kept it. Caboose from kabuis. Schooner from schoener. Sloop from sloep. Cruiser from kruiser. Snoop from snoepen. Spook from spook. Stoop from stoep, a doorstep. Poppycock from pappekak. If a homely, concrete word has a doubled o, Dutch is a strong first guess.

The sea vocabulary is almost all Dutch

The Dutch ran the seas in the seventeenth century and English took the words wholesale. Yacht from jacht. Sloop, schooner, cruiser, keelhaul, maelstrom, hoist, smuggle, freight, deck, skipper, dock, buoy, walrus, bow, avast. Yacht is worth its own note — j-a-c-h-t became y-a-c-h-t, keeping a Dutch ch that English does not pronounce at all.

The kitchen and the household too

Alongside the sea came the home. Cookie from koekje, a little cake — note the -je diminutive, which English flattened to -ie. Coleslaw from koolsalade, cabbage salad. Waffle from wafel. Gherkin from gurkijn. Brandy from brandewijn, burnt wine. Scone, easel from ezel meaning donkey, and offal. Plain words for plain things.

Dutch versus German — the tell

Both use sch-, both build compounds, and confusing them is easy. Dutch words in English are usually short and old and about ships, food or money — sloop, cookie, guilder, stiver, polder. German loans are usually longer, later, and about ideas or science — zeitgeist, schadenfreude, gestalt, doppelganger, bildungsroman. Length and subject matter separate them more reliably than spelling does.

Yiddish and Hebrew — two layers, one alphabet

The big idea
Two related but distinct sources, and telling them apart decides the spelling. Yiddish arrived through everyday speech in the last hundred and fifty years, so its words are informal and use German-style clusters: sch-, kn-, tz-. Hebrew arrived much earlier through scripture, so its words are formal and carry Hebrew grammar — the masculine plural -im, the feminine -oth, and a ch that stands for a sound English does not have. Together they account for over eight hundred words in the full library.
The pro move
CHUTZPAH
Heard. HOOTS-puh — extreme nerve Origin. Yiddish khutspe, from Hebrew hutspah Markers. the h sound is written ch; the ts is written tz; the final schwa is written a-h Spell. C H U T Z P A H Family. chutzpadik

Yiddish clusters

Yiddish freely begins words with clusters English otherwise avoids. Sch-: schlep, schmooze, schmaltz, schtick, schlock. Kn- with the k sounded: knish, knaidel. Ts written tz: chutzpah, kibitz, klutz, matzo, tchotchke. And a great many end in -el or -ke: bagel, dreidel, yarmulke, tchotchke. Recognise the cluster and you have the language.

Hebrew plurals are the giveaway

Hebrew brought its own grammar. The masculine plural is -im: seraphim, cherubim, kibbutzim, goyim, teraphim. The feminine plural is -oth or -ot: sephiroth, mitzvoth. So a word ending -im or -oth in a religious or scholarly context is almost certainly Hebrew, and the singular usually ends -ah or plain consonant: seraph, cherub, kibbutz.

The ch that is not a ch

Hebrew and Yiddish have a throat sound English lacks, and both are conventionally written ch. Chutzpah, challah, chanukah, chazan, brachah. It is never the ch of church and never the ch of chorus — it is closer to clearing your throat. When a Hebrew or Yiddish word begins with an h sound, ask whether it should be written ch.

Scripture put them in early

The Hebrew layer is a thousand years older than the Yiddish one, and it came through Bible translation, which is why those words feel formal. Jubilee, sabbath, shibboleth, behemoth, leviathan, messiah, jeremiad, cabal, hallelujah, amen, seraphim. Several arrived by way of Greek and Latin, which is why they look less foreign than the Yiddish ones do.

Russian and Slavic — suffixes that name things

The big idea
Slavic words entered English in identifiable waves — Tsarist court and countryside, then Soviet politics, then space and weapons — and they are unusually easy to spell once you know the suffixes, because Slavic languages build meaning by stacking endings that survive transliteration intact. The second useful fact is that Slavic tolerates consonant clusters English does not, so a word with an improbable pile of consonants at the start is a strong Slavic signal.
The pro move
PERESTROIKA
Heard. pair-uh-STROY-kuh — restructuring Parse. pere- (re-) + stroi (build) + -ka (noun ending) Markers. the oy sound is written o-i; the ending is -ka Spell. P E R E S T R O I K A Family. glasnost, apparatchik — the same Soviet wave

The suffixes do the work

-nik names a person or adherent: sputnik a fellow traveller, refusenik, beatnik, kibitznik. -ka makes a noun, often small or feminine: babushka, matryoshka, balalaika, troika, perestroika, dacha. -ov and -ev end surnames that became words: molotov, kalashnikov, stroganoff, pavlova. -ism and -ist attach to leaders: stalinism, leninism, trotskyism.

oi, not oy

Russian transliteration writes the oy sound as o-i. Perestroika, not perestroyka. Troika, not troyka. Coulibiac keeps its own oddity. This is a small point that decides several words, because English instinct reaches for o-y in this position — boy, toy, employ — and Slavic transliteration never does.

Consonant clusters English would refuse

Slavic languages happily begin words with clusters English avoids: zd-, vz-, chsh-, shch-. Chastushka carries a shch cluster in the middle. Borscht ends in three consonants. Sputnik, glasnost and gulag stack sounds English would break up with a vowel. If a word looks unpronounceable at the start or end, Slavic is a reasonable guess.

Three waves, three subjects

The oldest layer is rural and courtly: steppe, tundra, samovar, sable, kopeck, ruble, czar, vodka, kasha. The Soviet layer is political: commissar, gulag, apparatchik, glasnost, perestroika, agitprop, nomenklatura. The newest is technical and military: sputnik, cosmonaut, kalashnikov, molotov. Twenty-three sit in the core library and 438 in the full library.

Portuguese — the first global vocabulary

The big idea
Portuguese sailors reached Africa, Brazil, India, China and Japan before any other Europeans, and English took a great deal of its first global vocabulary from them second-hand. So a Portuguese loanword is often not originally Portuguese at all — it is a Tupi, Malay, Bantu or Konkani word wearing Portuguese spelling. That is the key insight: you spell the Portuguese route, not the ultimate source.
The pro move
ALBATROSS
Heard. AL-buh-tross Origin. Portuguese alcatraz, a pelican, reshaped by Latin albus meaning white Marker. Portuguese al- from Arabic, then an English respelling toward albus Spell. A L B A T R O S S Family. Alcatraz — the same word, unchanged

The -ão ending

Portuguese has a nasal ending written -ão, and English had no way to write it. It came out as -oon or -on. Galleon from galeão. Monsoon by way of monção. Festoon, dragoon and cartoon reached English through the same French and Portuguese route. When a word ends -oon and is about ships, weather or the tropics, suspect Portuguese.

Portuguese as a courier

Many of these words are only Portuguese in transit. Jacaranda and piranha are Tupi from Brazil. Cobra is Portuguese cobra de capelo, hooded snake. Mango is Tamil mankay, Portuguese manga. Teak is Malayalam tekka. Pagoda came through Portuguese pagode. Molasses from melaço. The Portuguese spelling is the one English copied, so that is the one to learn.

Brazilian words keep their shape

Words that came straight from Brazil are unusually well preserved. Capoeira, the martial art and dance. Jacaranda, the tree. Anhinga, the darter bird. Piranha, with its Portuguese nh for the ny sound. Feijoada, the bean stew. Samba. Note nh and lh — Portuguese writes the ny and ly sounds that way, where Spanish uses ñ and ll.

Portuguese versus Spanish

The two look alike and the tell is in a few letters. Portuguese uses nh and lh where Spanish uses ñ and ll: piranha against señor. Portuguese uses ç and ão, Spanish does not. Portuguese words in English cluster around Brazil, Africa and the sea routes to Asia; Spanish ones cluster around Mexico, the American Southwest and the Caribbean. Twenty-two are in the core library and 271 in the full library.

Celtic — Irish, Welsh and Gaelic spelling logic

The big idea
Celtic loanwords look impossible because Irish and Welsh use the Roman alphabet for sounds it was never designed for, and they solved that with letter pairs rather than new letters. Once you know what those pairs mean, the words stop being arbitrary. Irish adds an h to soften a consonant and inserts an i to signal the quality of the one before it. Welsh uses ll for a sound English lacks entirely, dd for th, and treats w and y as vowels.
The pro move
BODHRAN
Heard. BOW-rawn — an Irish frame drum Origin. Irish bodhrán, from bodhar meaning deaf or dull Marker. Irish d-h is lenited — the d goes silent, so you hear a vowel where letters sit Spell. B O D H R A N Family. bodhranai — the player

Irish lenition — the added h

Irish softens a consonant by writing an h after it, and the result often sounds nothing like the letters. bh and mh sound like v or w. dh and gh often go silent. th sounds like h. So bodhran sounds bow-rawn, taoiseach sounds TEE-shukh, and Siobhan sounds shiv-AWN. When an Irish word has a consonant plus h, expect to hear less than you see.

Irish slender vowels

Irish marks whether a consonant is slender or broad by the vowel written beside it, which is why Irish words carry vowels you do not pronounce. Taoiseach has a-o-i together. Aoife, Caoimhe, saoirse all use that a-o-i cluster. These vowels are grammar, not sound. Do not try to hear them — learn the cluster as a unit.

Welsh ll, dd and w

Welsh double l is a single sound made by blowing air past the tongue, and English has nothing like it — llan, Llewellyn, Llanfair. Welsh dd is the th of this. Welsh w is a vowel, giving cwm and crwth. Welsh y is also a vowel. That is why Welsh place names look vowel-less to English eyes: the vowels are w and y.

The words English actually took

Most Celtic loans are informal and vivid: hooligan, banshee, brogue, galore, smithereens, shillelagh, slew, whiskey from uisce beatha meaning water of life, leprechaun, colcannon, sporran, plaid, clan, slogan from sluagh-ghairm, a battle cry. Penguin may be Welsh pen gwyn, white head. Thirteen are in the core library and 292 in the full library.

Turkish and Persian — the road words

The big idea
The Silk Road and the Ottoman court pushed a long list of words westward, and nearly all of them reached English through at least one other language, which is why their spellings look inconsistent. Persian supplied poetry, gardens, textiles and chess; Turkish supplied the court, the military and the kitchen. The practical skill is recognising which language the word passed through last, because that decides whether you write k or c, and ee or i.
The pro move
SERENDIPITY
Heard. ser-un-DIP-ih-tee — a happy accident Origin. Serendip, the Persian name for Sri Lanka, plus an English suffix Note. coined by Horace Walpole from a Persian fairy tale, so the root is a place name Spell. S E R E N D I P I T Y Family. serendipitous

Persian building blocks

-stan means land: Afghanistan, Hindustan. -abad means city. bad- relates to wind, giving badgir a wind-catcher. Persian also gave English its chess vocabulary: checkmate from shah mat, the king is helpless; rook from rukh. And the garden words: paradise from pairidaeza, a walled enclosure; jasmine, lilac, tulip, azure from lazhward, spinach, julep, and candy from qand.

Turkish suffixes

-lik makes an abstract noun or a container. -ci or -ji marks a trade: the origin of words like the Ottoman title. And Turkish gave English the court and the camp: pasha, vizier, janissary, seraglio, horde from ordu meaning army camp, khan, fez from the city of Fez, kiosk from kösk a garden pavilion, and turban.

The food came with them

Yogurt from yoğurt, kebab, baklava, sherbet from şerbet, coffee by way of Turkish kahve from Arabic qahwa, and pilaf. Sherbet and syrup and shrub are all the same Arabic root sharba, a drink, arriving by three different routes — which is exactly why their spellings look unrelated.

Why the spellings look inconsistent

Almost none of these came straight into English. Kiosk came through French, which is why it ends -sk and not -shk. Turban came through French and Italian. Azure came through Latin and lost its initial l. Tulip came through French tulipe, itself from Turkish tülbend, a turban, because the flower resembled one. Trace the route and the spelling stops looking random. Twelve are in core and 294 in the full library.

Malay and the Austronesian words

The big idea
Malay was the trade language of the whole East Indies, so English took its Southeast Asian vocabulary mostly through Malay, often via Portuguese or Dutch shipping. The words are unusually easy to spell for two reasons: Malay syllables are simple, generally a consonant plus a vowel, and Malay tolerates almost no consonant clusters. So a Malay loanword tends to alternate consonant and vowel neatly all the way through.
The pro move
BINTURONG
Heard. BIN-tuh-rong — a bearcat of Southeast Asia Origin. Malay binturung Marker. simple syllables — bin, tu, rong — and the final n-g typical of Malay Spell. B I N T U R O N G Family. linsang, pangolin — the same regional set

Simple open syllables

Malay builds words from consonant-vowel syllables with almost no clusters, so the spelling follows the sound closely. Rattan, batik, sarong, sago, kapok, parang, proa, tempeh, durian. Read it aloud slowly and you can usually write it. That regularity is the opposite of French or Irish, and it is why these words reward careful listening rather than etymological knowledge.

The -ng ending

Malay ends a great many words in -ng, and English kept it. Sarong, gong, binturong, linsang, parang, orangutan from orang hutan meaning forest person. When you hear a final -ng on a tropical or animal word, Malay is a strong guess — and note that the g is written but the sound is a single nasal.

Which came through Dutch

The Dutch controlled the East Indies for three centuries, so some of these arrived in English wearing Dutch spelling. Batik, from Javanese, came through Dutch. Gutta-percha, from getah perca. Amok, from amuk, came through Portuguese. Ketchup, one of the great travellers, came from Hokkien through Malay kicap and then English. Knowing the courier explains the letters.

The animals are the competition words

The bee-level vocabulary here is mostly natural history. Binturong, linsang, pangolin, babirusa, dugong, cassowary, cockatoo, gecko, mangosteen, rambutan, casuarina, pandanus. All simply spelled, all unfamiliar. Twelve are in the core library and 259 in the full library, and the full-library set is where the real drill value sits.

Chinese and Korean — two romanisations, one language

The big idea
The hardest thing about Chinese loanwords is that English has used two rival romanisation systems, so the same syllable can be spelled two entirely different ways depending on when the word arrived. Older borrowings use Wade-Giles or the older postal spellings; newer ones use Pinyin. Knowing which system produced a word is the whole spelling decision. Korean adds a third system with its own conventions.
The pro move
CHEONGSAM
Heard. chong-SAM — a fitted dress Origin. Cantonese cheuhngsaam, a long gown Marker. Cantonese romanisation keeps the eo vowel cluster English would not use Spell. C H E O N G S A M Family. samfu — the related garment

Wade-Giles against Pinyin

The same sounds, two systems. Wade-Giles wrote ch where Pinyin writes zh or j. Wade-Giles ts is Pinyin z or c. Wade-Giles hs is Pinyin x. Wade-Giles k is often Pinyin g. So Peking became Beijing, Mao Tse-tung became Mao Zedong, Taoism became Daoism, and Chungking became Chongqing. Both spellings still appear in dictionaries.

Which words came in when

Old borrowings kept their old spellings even after Pinyin arrived, which is why the vocabulary looks mixed. Tea, typhoon, kowtow, ketchup, tycoon, sampan, oolong, souchong, pekoe, litchi, kumquat, ginseng and kaolin all predate Pinyin. Renminbi, pinyin, qi, Beijing and Chongqing came in with it. Old and homely means the old system.

Cantonese did a lot of the work

Much early borrowing happened through the southern ports, so the words reflect Cantonese rather than Mandarin. Cheongsam, wok, chop suey, dim sum, bok choy, kumquat, loquat and souchong are Cantonese. If a food or clothing word feels southern, expect Cantonese vowels — the eo of cheongsam, the oy of choy.

Korean has its own system

Korean romanisation gives you eo, eu and ae for vowels English writes otherwise. Bibimbap, mixed rice. Naengmyeon, cold noodles. Taekwondo. Hangul, the alphabet. Kimchi. Bulgogi. Note the doubled vowels in naengmyeon — those are single Korean vowels written with two letters. Four Chinese and Korean words are in core, 175 in the full library.

Hawaiian and Polynesian — thirteen letters, no clusters

The big idea
Hawaiian has the smallest alphabet of any language English borrows from: five vowels, seven consonants, and the glottal stop. Every syllable ends in a vowel and no two consonants ever touch. That single fact makes Hawaiian words the most predictable in the entire course — if you can hear the syllables, you can spell the word, because there is no other legal way to write it. The same rules broadly hold across Polynesian languages.
The pro move
PAHOEHOE
Heard. puh-HOY-hoy — smooth, ropy lava Origin. Hawaiian pāhoehoe Marker. every syllable ends in a vowel — pa, ho, e, ho, e — so no consonant can double Spell. P A H O E H O E Family. aa — the rough lava, the shortest word in the set

The whole alphabet

Hawaiian uses a, e, i, o, u and h, k, l, m, n, p, w, plus the glottal stop written as an apostrophe. That is it. There is no b, c, d, f, g, j, q, r, s, t, v, x, y or z. So the moment you know a word is Hawaiian, three quarters of the alphabet is eliminated — a stronger constraint than any rule elsewhere in this course.

Every syllable ends in a vowel

No Hawaiian syllable ends in a consonant and no two consonants ever sit together. Pahoehoe, kipuka, wahine, haupia, laulau, kakapo, muumuu, ukulele, humuhumunukunukuapuaa. Say it slowly, write consonant-vowel, consonant-vowel, and you will be right. Doubled vowels are not a mistake — aa, aalii, muumuu are all real.

The famous long one

Humuhumunukunukuapuaa is the Hawaiian triggerfish, and it is 21 letters of pure pattern: humu humu nuku nuku a puaa. Two doubled words, a connector, and a word meaning pig — the fish was said to grunt like one. It is in your library, and it is far easier than a 21-letter Latin word, because every syllable is legal in only one way.

Wider Polynesian

Related languages behave similarly with slightly larger alphabets. Maori gives tuatara and moa and allows t and r. Tongan and Samoan give tapa and kava. Tuatara, kakapo and moa come from New Zealand. Taboo is Tongan tabu and tattoo is Tahitian tatau — both reached English through Captain Cook's voyages. Eight of these are in core and 82 in the full library.

African languages — Afrikaans, Bantu and the rest

The big idea
Two very different sources under one heading. Afrikaans is a daughter of Dutch, so it compounds the same way and its words are transparent once you know a handful of elements. The Bantu languages work by prefixes: a noun's class is marked at the front, so the m, n, ki or vu you hear at the start of a word is grammar rather than part of the root. Recognising a prefix as a prefix is what makes these spellable.
The pro move
AARDWOLF
Heard. ARD-woolf — a small striped hyena Origin. Afrikaans aardwolf, earth wolf Marker. Afrikaans doubles the a for a long vowel — aard, from Dutch aarde meaning earth Spell. A A R D W O L F Family. aardvark, aasvogel — the same doubled a

Afrikaans elements

Learn a short list and the compounds open up. aard- earth: aardvark earth-pig, aardwolf earth-wolf. -veld field: veld, bushveld, highveld. -kop head or hill: koppie. -bok buck: springbok, steenbok, gemsbok, reebok. -slang snake: boomslang, tree-snake. aas- carrion: aasvogel, a vulture. The doubled a is Afrikaans marking a long vowel, exactly as Dutch does.

Bantu prefixes are grammar

Bantu languages mark a noun's class with a prefix, and English often swallowed the prefix into the word. Swahili safari is a journey; simba a lion; bwana a master. Kalimba and marimba both carry an instrument prefix. Vuvuzela, jenga meaning to build, and impala all begin with a class marker. Do not try to find a root in those first letters — they are grammar.

The words that travelled by ship

A separate route brought West African words to the Americas and back into English through the Caribbean and the American South. Banjo, banana, jazz, jumbo, goober meaning peanut, gumbo, jambalaya, tote, yam, okra, voodoo, zombie. These arrived without their original spellings and got English ones, so they look native — which is exactly why their origin surprises people.

Where to expect them

Afrikaans words in English are almost all natural history and landscape: the animals of the veld and the shape of the land. Bantu words are mostly music, food and social terms. Twenty-eight of these are in the core library and 111 in the full library. And apartheid is worth its own note — Afrikaans apart plus heid, matching English -hood, so it means apart-hood.

Native American — the -tl that gives it away

The big idea
The clearest fingerprint in this chapter belongs to Nahuatl, the language of the Aztecs: it permits a t-l cluster that English finds nearly unpronounceable, and it uses it constantly at the end of words. Everything else in the group mostly arrived through Spanish, French or English colonists, who reshaped the words to fit their own spelling, which is why words like raccoon and skunk look completely English despite being Algonquian.
The pro move
AXOLOTL
Heard. AK-suh-lot-ul — a salamander that never grows up Origin. Nahuatl axolotl, from atl water plus xolotl a servant or monster Marker. the Nahuatl t-l ending, and x for a sh-like sound Spell. A X O L O T L Family. atlatl, quetzal, nopales — all Nahuatl

Nahuatl -tl

Nahuatl ends a great many words in t-l, and English kept the letters even though it cannot really pronounce them. Atlatl a spear-thrower, axolotl, quetzal from quetzalli, nahuatl itself, ocelotl giving ocelot, tomatl giving tomato, ahuacatl giving avocado. When a word about Mexico ends in a t-l cluster, or once did, it is Nahuatl.

Spanish softened most of them

Nahuatl reached English through Spanish, which trimmed the endings to fit its own patterns. Tomatl became tomate then tomato. Ahuacatl became aguacate then avocado. Chocolatl became chocolate. Xocolatl kept its x, pronounced sh. Coyotl became coyote. So the English form is usually the Spanish reshaping, not the Nahuatl original.

The Algonquian words look English

Along the Atlantic coast, English colonists borrowed and then thoroughly anglicised. Raccoon, skunk, moose, opossum, squash, hickory, hominy, pecan, persimmon, succotash, terrapin, moccasin, powwow, toboggan, wigwam, wampum, caucus. Because English speakers did the respelling, these carry no foreign marker at all — which is what makes their origin a surprise rather than a spelling problem.

Quechua and the Andes

A third group came from Quechua through Spanish: llama, alpaca, vicuna, condor, guano, pampa, quinine from quina, quinoa, jerky from charqui, puma, coca. Note llama with its double l, which is Spanish spelling, and quinoa which keeps a Quechua vowel sequence. Two of these are in the core library and 46 in the full library.
