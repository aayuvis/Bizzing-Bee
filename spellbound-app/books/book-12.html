Advanced Orthography

-esce, -escent, -escence — the becoming words

The big idea
Latin had a whole verb class for beginning to be something: -escere, called the inchoative. English inherited the trio intact. The verb ends -esce, the adjective -escent, the noun -escence. Because the sound is just ess, spellers reach for -ess or -esent, and because the noun ends in a soft s sound they reach for -essence. The c never leaves and never doubles, and knowing the trio means one word gives you three.
The pro move
ERUBESCENT
Heard. er-oo-BESS-unt — beginning to redden Origin. Latin rubescere, from ruber = red, plus -escere = beginning to Rule. process starting → e s c; adjective takes -escent Spell. E R U B E S C E N T Family. erubescence, rubescent, rubicund

One root, three words

Learn the trio and you triple your return. Coalesce, coalescent, coalescence. Obsolesce, obsolescent, obsolescence. Effervesce, effervescent, effervescence. Convalesce, convalescent, convalescence. Luminesce, luminescent, luminescence. The moment you can spell any one member, you can spell the other two.

The c is doing real work

That c is not decoration. It is the visible remains of Latin -escere, and it is what tells you the word means a process getting under way rather than a finished state. Compare senescent, growing old, with senile, already old. Compare liquescent, becoming liquid, with liquid. The c marks the becoming.

Colour and light words cluster here

A surprising number of these describe something changing shade or glow, because Latin loved forming them from colour adjectives. Albescent, turning white. Canescent, turning grey. Nigrescent, turning black. Flavescent, turning yellow. Rubescent and erubescent, turning red. Iridescent, taking on rainbow colours. Virescent, turning green. If the definition says turning some colour, expect -escent.

Where the traps are

Three slips account for nearly all errors. Writing -essent for -escent, which loses the Latin marker. Writing -essence for -escence, which confuses it with the unrelated word essence. And doubling the s in the verb, as in coalessce. The c stays single, the s stays single, and they always appear in that order. Sixty-seven of these words sit in the core library, a hundred and fifteen in the full.

Advanced Orthography

-efy or -ify — only four take the e

The big idea
Both endings come from Latin facere, to make, and both are pronounced the same. English standardised on -ify for essentially the whole language — and then left exactly four verbs behind with -efy. That is the entire exception list, which makes this one of the few patterns you can master completely in one sitting. The noun forms are the second half of the story, because -efy words take -efaction, not -ification.
The pro move
LIQUEFY
Heard. LICK-wuh-fye — to make liquid Candidates. -efy or -ify Rule. the only four with e are liquefy, rarefy, putrefy, stupefy Spell. L I Q U E F Y Noun. liquefaction — never liquification

The complete exception list

Liquefy, rarefy, putrefy, stupefy. That is all of them. Every other English verb in this family takes -ify: classify, magnify, terrify, justify, testify, ratify, mollify, nullify, vilify, edify, codify, deify, typify, verify, purify, qualify, amplify, glorify. Four words against hundreds, and the four are the ones examiners ask.

The noun exposes the verb

This is the elegant part. The four -efy verbs form nouns in -efaction: liquefaction, rarefaction, putrefaction, stupefaction. Every -ify verb forms a noun in -ification: classification, magnification, justification. So if you can remember the noun, the verb follows automatically — and if you can remember the verb, you already know the noun.

Where -faction comes from

Latin facere gave a noun factio, an act of making. That is why the noun has an a where the verb has none: liquefy but liquefaction, satisfy but satisfaction. The f-a-c is the visible root, meaning make, and it also shows up in factory, manufacture, benefaction and malefaction. Same root, doing the same job.

Why this pattern earns its hour

Our core library holds 142 words ending in this family and the full library 364, so the -ify side is enormous. But the value is concentrated: because -ify is the default, the only thing you actually have to store is the list of four. Learn four words and a rule about nouns, and a 364-word pattern becomes safe.

Advanced Orthography

Bearing, eating, birthing — iferous, ivorous, parous, fugal, fluous

The big idea
Five Latin verbs became productive endings, and they behave as a family. Ferre, to bear, gives -iferous. Vorare, to devour, gives -ivorous. Parere, to bring forth, gives -parous. Fugere, to flee, gives -fugal. Fluere, to flow, gives -fluous. The spelling problem is almost never the ending itself but the vowel that joins it to the root, and that vowel is i far more often than anything else.
The pro move
FOSSILIFEROUS
Heard. foss-ill-IFF-er-us — bearing fossils Parse. fossil + i + ferous Rule. Latin joins root to ending with a connecting i, not an a or an o Spell. F O S S I L I F E R O U S Family. carboniferous, cupriferous, auriferous

The five endings and their verbs

-iferous, from ferre, to bear or carry: coniferous bears cones, auriferous bears gold, fossiliferous bears fossils. -ivorous, from vorare, to devour: carnivorous, herbivorous, piscivorous, insectivorous. -parous, from parere, to bring forth: oviparous lays eggs, viviparous bears live young, multiparous has borne several. -fugal, from fugere, to flee: centrifugal flees the centre, lucifugal flees light. -fluous, from fluere, to flow: mellifluous flows like honey, superfluous overflows.

The connecting vowel is i

This is where the marks are lost. Latin joined a noun root to a verb ending with a short connecting vowel, and in this family it is almost always i. Cupriferous, not cupraferous. Plumbiferous, not plumbeferous. Laticiferous. Cruciferous. Nitriferous. When you hear the ending, assume i and then look for a reason to think otherwise.

The metals give you a free set

Mineralogy loves -iferous, and the roots are the Latin metal names you may already know. Aurum gold gives auriferous. Argentum silver gives argentiferous. Cuprum copper gives cupriferous. Plumbum lead gives plumbiferous. Ferrum iron gives ferriferous. Stannum tin gives stanniferous. Learn six metals and you have earned six long words.

Where the family breaks pattern

A few members join with a different vowel because the Latin root already ended in one. Omnivorous keeps the i of omni. Septemfluous keeps the e of septem, seven. Frugivorous keeps the i of frugi. And a small group uses -ferous without the i where the root supplies its own vowel, as in odiferous beside odoriferous. Fifty-five of these sit in core and a hundred and eleven in the full library.

Advanced Orthography

-ance or -ence after soft c and g

The big idea
English spells the same unstressed ending two ways, and after a c or g the choice is not arbitrary at all — it is forced by pronunciation. A c or g stays soft only when the next letter is e, i or y. So if you hear a soft sound just before the ending, the ending must begin with e. If you hear a hard sound, an a is available. This single piece of phonics resolves a whole class of words that otherwise have to be memorised one at a time.
The pro move
INSURGENCE
Heard. in-SUR-junce — the g is soft, like j Rule. a soft g needs e, i or y after it to stay soft Test. insurgance would force a hard g, giving in-sur-GANCE Spell. I N S U R G E N C E Family. insurgent, insurgency, resurgence

Why the rule exists

In English a c before a, o or u is hard, as in cat, cot, cut. Before e, i or y it is soft, as in cent, city, cycle. The same holds for g: gate and got are hard, gem and gin are soft. So the vowel after the letter is not decoration, it is the instruction that tells you how to pronounce the letter before it.

Apply it to the ending

Say the word and listen to the consonant just before the ending. Soft, like j or s? Then the ending must start with e. Negligence, intelligence, diligence, indulgence, divergence, emergence, reticence, innocence, beneficence, magnificence. Hard? Then a is allowed. Arrogance, elegance, extravagance, significance, vacancy — note that in significance and vacancy the c is hard, a k sound.

The -ency and -ancy nouns follow

The same logic governs the shorter noun forms. Soft g gives -gency: emergency, exigency, stringency, insurgency, plangency, tangency. Hard c gives -cancy: vacancy, mendicancy. And the adjective agrees too — insurgent, divergent, indulgent all keep the e for the same reason the noun does.

What this does not settle

Be honest about the limits. The rule only bites when the letter before the ending is c or g. For everything else — resistance against existence, persistence against assistance — you still need the Latin conjugation or plain memory. But c and g cover 65 words in the core library and 119 in the full library, and those are exactly the ones that look unguessable until you know the trick.

Who rules — archy, cracy, arch and the government words

The big idea
Two Greek roots produce almost every word for a form of government, and they are not interchangeable. Arkhein meant to rule or to be first, and gives -arch for the ruler and -archy for the system. Kratos meant strength or power, and gives -crat for the person and -cracy for the system. The spelling trap is the ch: it is a Greek chi, always ch and never k, and the same root sits inside architect, archive and archaeology.
The pro move
KAKISTOCRACY
Heard. kack-iss-TOCK-ruh-see — government by the worst people Parse. kakistos = worst, plus o, plus kratos = power Rule. a group holding power → cracy; the connecting vowel is o in Greek Spell. K A K I S T O C R A C Y Family. kakistocrat, kakistocratic

Ruler versus system

-arch names a person: monarch, patriarch, matriarch, oligarch, ecclesiarch, gymnasiarch, heresiarch. -archy names the system that person heads: monarchy, oligarchy, patriarchy, anarchy, autarchy, gynarchy. -crat names a person too — democrat, technocrat, autocrat — and -cracy names their system. Deciding person or system chooses your ending before you write a letter.

The ch is a Greek chi

Every one of the -arch words spells the k sound c-h, because the Greek letter was chi. Monarch, anarchy, hierarchy, archetype, architect, archaeology, archive, archaic. There is no monark or anarky. The moment you hear a k sound in a word about ruling, being first or being ancient, write ch.

The connecting o

Greek joined a root to kratos with an o, and that o is the letter people drop. Theocracy, not theacracy. Ochlocracy, from ochlos, a crowd. Technocracy, from techne, skill. Plutocracy, from ploutos, wealth. Gerontocracy, from geron, an old man. Kakistocracy, from kakistos, worst. Assume the joint is o.

How productive it is

This pattern is still generating words, which is why the library is full of unusual ones: squattocracy, punditocracy, kindergarchy, cryptocracy, ergatocracy, mobocracy, adhocracy, e-democracy. Sixty-one sit in the core competition list and a hundred and fifty-two in the full library. If a definition names a group and says they hold the power, build it with o-c-r-a-c-y and you will usually be right.

Advanced Orthography

-itude — the quality-of-being nouns

The big idea
Latin -itudo turned an adjective into a noun naming the quality itself: from altus, high, came altitudo, height. English kept the suffix as -itude and it now names abstract qualities across the language. Two things trip spellers. The connecting vowel is i, not a, so it is never -atude. And the adjective is built on the Latin oblique stem itudin-, giving -itudinous and -itudinal, which is why longitude becomes longitudinal rather than longitudal.
The pro move
PULCHRITUDE
Heard. PULL-kruh-tood — physical beauty Origin. Latin pulcher = beautiful, plus -itudo = the quality of Trap. the ch is a Latin ch, said like k Spell. P U L C H R I T U D E Family. pulchritudinous

How the noun is built

Take a Latin adjective, add -itudo, get the quality. Altus high gives altitude. Latus wide gives latitude. Longus long gives longitude. Certus sure gives certitude. Aptus fit gives aptitude. Gratus pleasing gives gratitude. Servus a slave gives servitude. The connecting vowel is i every time — there is no -atude in English.

The stem is itudin-

In Latin the suffix declined, and its stem was itudin-. That stem is what English uses for further derivatives, which is why the adjectives look longer than expected. Longitude gives longitudinal. Latitude gives latitudinal. Attitude gives attitudinal. Multitude gives multitudinous. Pulchritude gives pulchritudinous. Vicissitude gives vicissitudinous. Never longitudal, never multitudous.

The hard ones and their roots

The competition words in this family are all transparent once you have the Latin. Pulchritude from pulcher, beautiful. Turpitude from turpis, shameful. Vicissitude from vicis, a turn or change. Lassitude from lassus, weary. Decrepitude from decrepitus, broken down. Solicitude from sollicitus, anxious. Ineptitude from ineptus, unsuitable. Rectitude from rectus, straight.

Small pattern, high hit rate

Only 35 of these are in the core library and 61 in the full library, so this is a small family. But its members appear at the hard end of word lists out of proportion to their number, because the roots are unusual Latin adjectives and the -itudinous derivatives are long. A short list worth owning completely.

-mancy — divination by absolutely anything

The big idea
Greek manteia meant prophecy or divination, and English used it to name a method of foretelling by any object at all. It is one of the most reliably spellable families on a bee stage: the ending never varies, the connecting vowel is o, and the root is a plain Greek noun for whatever you are reading the future in. Once you know the roots, words nobody has ever seen become buildable.
The pro move
AILUROMANCY
Heard. eye-LOOR-uh-man-see — divination by watching cats Parse. ailouros = cat, plus o, plus manteia = prophecy Rule. the joint is o; the ending is m a n c y Spell. A I L U R O M A N C Y Family. ailuromantic, ailurophile, ailurophobia

The three forms

The method is -mancy: necromancy, pyromancy, chiromancy. The practitioner is -mancer: necromancer, geomancer. The adjective is -mantic: necromantic, chiromantic. All three keep the m-a-n, because Greek manteia had an a. There is no -mency and no -mantik.

Learn the roots and build the words

Every one of these is a Greek noun plus the suffix. Cheir hand gives chiromancy, palm reading. Ge earth gives geomancy. Pyr fire gives pyromancy. Selene moon gives selenomancy. Oneiros dream gives oneiromancy. Ichthys fish gives ichthyomancy. Biblion book gives bibliomancy. Aleuron flour gives aleuromancy. Daktylos finger gives dactylomancy.

The strange ones are the best ones

The library is full of methods nobody practises any more, which makes them perfect competition words. Enoptromancy, by mirror. Arachnomancy, by spiders. Cephalomancy, by a skull. Maculomancy, by spots or blemishes. Crithomancy, by barley. Lecanomancy, by a basin of water. Botanomancy, by burning plants. Ailuromancy, by the behaviour of cats.

Do not confuse it with romantic

Two words end in these letters without belonging to the family. Romantic comes from Rome by way of the Romance languages and has nothing to do with prophecy. Semantic comes from Greek sema, a sign. Dormancy comes from Latin dormire, to sleep. None of them takes a -mancer form, which is your test. Thirty of the real ones are in core, forty-six in the full library.
