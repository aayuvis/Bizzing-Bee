Chapter 1 · in- / im- / il- / ir- (not / without)The idea · ★★☆
The big idea
Latin in- = not. It assimilates — changes shape — to match whatever consonant follows. This doubling is both a spelling signal and a meaning signal: the doubled letter reveals the hidden prefix.
The pro move
IRREFUTABLE
Say it. ih-ruh-FYOO-tuh-buhl
Spell it. I · R · R · E · F · U · T · A · B · L · E
Say it again. ih-ruh-FYOO-tuh-buhl
Syllables. ih · rah · fyuw · tah · bah · l
Sounds → Letters. [ih=IR] [REF=REF] [yuh=U] [tuh=TA] [bul=BLE]

The Assimilation Rule

in- = not. Before l → il-: illegal, illegible, illicit. Before m/b/p → im-: impossible, immoral, impeccable. Before r → ir-: irregular, irrefutable, irrevocable.

High-Value im- Words

Impeccable (im- + pecca = sin) = without flaw. Imperturbable = cannot be disturbed. Impecunious (im- + pecunia = money) = penniless. Impetuous = without restraint, rash.

Vex alert
IMPECUNious means no PECUNIA (money)

Chapter 2 · un- (not / reverse action)The idea · ★☆☆
The big idea
Old English/Latin un- = not (before adjectives) or reverse the action of (before verbs). The same two letters, two completely different jobs — word class determines meaning.
The pro move
UNCONSCIONABLE
Say it. uh-n-k-AH-n-sh-uh-n-uh-b-uh-l
Spell it. U · N · C · O · N · S · C · I · O · N · A · B · L · E
Say it again. uh-n-k-AH-n-sh-uh-n-uh-b-uh-l
Syllables. u · n · c · o · n · s · c · i · o · n · a · b · l · e
Sounds → Letters. [un=UN] [KON=CON] [shuh=SCION] [uh=A] [bul=BLE]

Two Jobs

un- before an adjective = "not": unkempt (not combed), unrequited (not returned), unprecedented (not before done). un- before a verb = "reverse": undo, unwrap, unravel.

High-Value un- Adjectives

Unkempt (un- + kempt = combed). Unfettered (un- + fetter = chain) = not restrained. Unimpeachable = beyond reproach. Unscrupulous = without moral principles. Unwieldy = hard to handle.

Chapter 3 · dis- (apart / not / opposite)The idea · ★★☆
The big idea
Latin dis- = apart, not, or deprived of. When it meets a root beginning with s, the result is double-s. Recognizing this doubling is the key to spelling dissipate, dissolve, and dissemble correctly.
The pro move
DISINGENUOUS
Say it. dih-sih-NJEH-nyoo-uhs
Spell it. D · I · S · I · N · G · E · N · U · O · U · S
Say it again. dih-sih-NJEH-nyoo-uhs
Syllables. dih · sih · njheh · nyuw · ah · s
Sounds → Letters. [dis=DIS] [in=IN] [JEN=GEN] [yoo=U] [us=OUS]

What dis- Does

dis- = apart, not, or deprive of. Dissonance (dis- + sonus = sound apart) = harsh discord. Discern (dis- + cernere = separate) = separate truth from error.

dis- + s-root = double-s

When the root begins with s, you get dis- + s- = diss-: dissipate (scatter apart), dissolve, dissent, dissertation, dissemble, disseminate (dis- + semen = seed = scatter seeds of ideas).

Vex alert
DIS-IN-GENU-OUS hides the Latin for 'genuine' — someone disingenuous is NOT genuine.

Chapter 4 · de- (down / away / reverse)The idea · ★★☆
The big idea
Latin de- removes, reverses, takes down, or completely intensifies. It is one of the most stable Latin prefixes — it never assimilates. The challenge is always what comes after de-.
The pro move
DESICCATE
Say it. d-EH-s-uh-k-ay-t
Spell it. D · E · S · I · C · C · A · T · E
Say it again. d-EH-s-uh-k-ay-t
Syllables. d · e · s · i · c · c · a · t · e
Sounds → Letters. [DES=DES] [ik=IC] [kayt=CATE]

What de- Does

de- removes (decaffeinate, defrost), reverses (decode, decompose), goes down (descend, decline), or means thoroughly (devout, demolish). Desiccate = de- + siccus (dry) = remove all moisture.

de- Removing Words

Decimate = de- + decimus (tenth) = originally kill one in ten (Roman military punishment); now: destroy a large portion. Denigrate = de- + niger (black) = blacken someone's reputation.
