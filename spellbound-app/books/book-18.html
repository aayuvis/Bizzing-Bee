The big idea
Working memory holds only about four items, so a 14-letter word must become 3-5 blocks, not 14 loose letters. This is one of the thirty-six techniques the last continent is built on, and it is the whole of this chapter: read it, run the drill, then use it on every list you touch afterwards.
The pro move
CHUNKING INTO SYLLABLE BLOCKS
1. Say the word slowly and split it at natural syllable breaks into 3-5 speakable chunks.
2. Write each chunk in its own box so the word becomes a short list of blocks.
3. Flag any chunk with doubled or silent letters as a 'danger block' to drill separately.
4. Rehearse the blocks in order, then recombine them into the full word.
In practice. 'Sesquipedalian' becomes ses | qui | ped | al | ian — five sayable blocks instead of fourteen stray letters; drill 'ses' and 'qui' as danger blocks.
