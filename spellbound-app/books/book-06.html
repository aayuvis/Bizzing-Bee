Chapter 1 · aud (hear)The idea · ★★☆
The big idea
Latin audire = to hear. Everything about hearing and listening uses this root — from the audience that listens to audacious daring (a false friend: same letters, different root!).
The pro move
AUDITORIUM
Say it. aw-duh-TAW-ree-uhm Spell it. A · U · D · I · T · O · R · I · U · M Say it again. aw-duh-TAW-ree-uhm Syllables. ao · dah · tao · riy · ah · m Sounds → Letters. [aw=AU] [dih=DI] [TOR=TOR] [ee=I] [um=UM] Exception. audire = to hear; -um = Latin neuter ending; 5 syllables; aw-dih-TOR-ee-um Origin. Latin Root. Latin auditorium = place for hearing

The Root

Latin audire = to hear. Audible = able to be heard. Audience = those who come to hear. Auditorium = a place for hearing. Audit = originally an oral examination (hearing accounts). Audiovisual = combining sound and sight. All hearing-related — all from aud-.

The Audacious Trap

⚠️ Audacious shares aud- BUT comes from audere = to dare (different Latin root!). Audible (hearing) ≠ audacious (daring). Both share A-U-D at the start. The definition is the only way to know which root you are dealing with. This is a famous bee confusable.

aud- in Legal/Formal Contexts

Audit = an official hearing/examination of financial records. Auditor = one who audits. Judicial — NOT from aud-. Inaudible (in- + audible) = cannot be heard. Audition = a hearing/trial performance. Audiometry (aud- + -metry) = measurement of hearing.

Subaudition

A rare but beautiful word: subaudition (sub- + audit + -ion) = understanding something that is not explicitly stated — the meaning you "hear under" the words. Also: subaudible = below the threshold of hearing. These show how aud- combines with other prefixes.

Vex alert
AUDIBLE uses -IBLE not -ABLE: you need your EAR (AUD) to make it audIBLE — 'I' hear it!

Chapter 2 · dict / dic (say / speak)The idea · ★★☆
The big idea
Latin dicere = to say. One of the most productive roots in legal, political, and everyday English. Every time something is said officially, pronounced, or declared, dict- is likely present.
The pro move
MALEDICTION
Say it. mal-eh-DIK-shun Spell it. M · A · L · E · D · I · C · T · I · O · N Say it again. mal-eh-DIK-shun Syllables. male · dic · tion Sounds → Letters. [mal=MAL] [uh=E] [DIK=DIC] [shun=TION] Exception. mal- = bad; -dict- = say; -ion = noun; contrast with benediction = good saying; stress: mal-uh-DIK-shun Origin. Latin Root. Latin: maledico (Latin)

The Root

Latin dicere = to say. Diction = the way one says words = enunciation. Dictate = say officially. Edict (e- = out + dict) = what is said officially = a decree. Indict (in- + dict) = formally accuse (the c is silent in English: /in-DYTE/!). Verdict (ver = truth + dict) = what is truly said by a jury.

Bene- and Male-diction

Benediction (bene + dict + -ion) = a good saying = a blessing. Malediction (male + dict + -ion) = a bad saying = a curse. These are structural opposites built from the same dict- root. In a bee: benediction = B-E-N-E-D-I-C-T-I-O-N; malediction = M-A-L-E-D-I-C-T-I-O-N.

Contradict & Predict

Contradict (contra- = against + dict) = say against = dispute. Predict (pre- + dict) = say before = forecast. Interdict (inter- + dict) = say between/forbid. Valediction (vale = farewell + dict) = a farewell saying. Jurisdiction (juris = law + dict) = saying what the law is = legal authority.

Abdicate vs Dedicate

⚠️ Neither abdicate nor dedicate is from dicere = say. Abdicate comes from abdicare = renounce. Dedicate = dedicare = set apart for a purpose. These look like dict- words but use a different Latin root dicare (not dicere). The definition at the microphone resolves the confusion.

Vex alert
Drop the E before adding -ING: DEDICATING, not dedicateing.
