Birds of every feather 1 of 4

Wings, songs and a surprising amount of scorn.

⬡ as bald as a coot
completely bald
Grandpa jokes he's as bald as a coot in every family photo.
The coot, a water bird, has a bare white patch on its forehead; recorded since the 1400s.
⬡ as bald as an eagle
completely bald
The coach is as bald as an eagle.
American; the bald eagle isn't bald — 'balde' meant white-headed in old English.
⬡ as black as a raven
glossy deep black, especially hair
The heroine in her book had hair as black as a raven.
From the Bible's Song of Solomon, where the beloved's hair is 'black as a raven'.
⬡ as cheerful as a lark
very happy and lively
Dad is as cheerful as a lark on pancake Saturdays.
Larks pour out song at first light, which is also why early risers are 'up with the lark.'
⬡ as crazy as a loon
wildly silly or foolish
My little brother acts as crazy as a loon after two cupcakes.
The loon's wailing, laughing night call sounds unhinged — though 'loon' may also echo the old word 'lunatic', so the origin is tangled.
⬡ as dead as a dodo
completely extinct or obsolete
Fax machines are as dead as a dodo.
The dodo of Mauritius was wiped out by 1681, becoming extinction's mascot.
⬡ as free as a bird
completely free
School's out — I'm as free as a bird.
Birds embody freedom because they can simply fly away.
⬡ as fussy as a hen with one chick
overly worried about every small thing
Mom gets as fussy as a hen with one chick before the spelling bee.
A hen with a single chick guards it constantly; recorded in Brewer's Dictionary of Phrase and Fable.
