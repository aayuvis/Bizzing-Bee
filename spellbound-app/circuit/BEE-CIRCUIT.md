## 0. The season in one page

**What it is.** A spelling season told on top of the Word Atlas. Each Atlas region ends at a **Bee Hall**, where the child spells a regional round against the ten rivals Bee already has: Pip, Nova, Rafi, Suki, Dax, Mira, Theo, Ines, Kwame and Vesper. Between rounds, **Surprise Stops** break out. Something goes wrong on the circuit, the rivals put the rivalry aside, and the team solves the case using that region's spelling theme. The story arrives through the **Hive Post**: invitations, programmes, rivals' letters, the Pronouncer's scouting report, and the notes.

**Three engines drive it.**

1. **The antagonist: Dax.** He is out in the open, eleven, and "fastest in round one, ask anyone". He decides on day one that the child is the speller who can beat him, and he plays dirty to stop it. Kid-safe dirty means:
   - psych-outs and fake "tips" whispered in the corridor;
   - rumours, and getting rivals blamed;
   - hiding the spare bell to delay a round;
   - starting the rumour that could get Suki's round struck off.

   He crosses a line when Kwame cracks, and he ends up alone with a formal warning from the Committee. At the Finish Arch he fades exactly as his tell says. Kwame, the speller he mocked, sits down next to him. That is the turn. Dax reforms, clears Vesper in the last case, and **captains the Circuit to victory in the Team Bee** against Ravenmere Academy.
2. **The mystery: who is "— V."?** After every round a short, strange note arrives where nobody could have put it. It is signed only **— V.** Each note is a riddle that knows the child's weakness *and* predicts what happens next: *"The bell will ring twice tonight. Listen for the second. — V."* The child keeps a **Suspect Board** all season:
   - Vesper, the silent champion who writes in violet;
   - the Pronouncer, V. Abara, left-handed;
   - Ratchet the Raccoon, who "borrows" things;
   - Dax, everyone's first guess, because he's the villain.

   Dax is cleared at his reform: V. had been writing to him too, and he ignored every note. After the final the answer comes: **the Pronouncer**, who placed last as the newest speller decades ago and has written to the newest speller every season since. **The sting:** after Team Night one more note arrives, in a *different* hand, while the Pronouncer is standing beside the child. *"There were always two of us. — V."* That carries the mystery into Season 2.
3. **The suspense: Vesper's silence.** Last year's champion looks at no one and asks for nothing. She is the false solution in Case 5, and in the final's blackout she asks her first question ever.

**Season 2 hook.** Ravenmere Academy, polished, undefeated and condescending ("Circuit kids learn on a map"), loses Team Night to Dax's team. Their captain, Sloane, sends the last letter of the season: *"Next season. Our hall. Our list."* **Ravenmere is Season 2's antagonist camp.** Its youngest speller, Mei, slips the child a note of her own. That second hand may be V.'s other writer.

**What does not change.** Stops, cards, sets, spelling levels, order, unlocks and the Atlas route. A child who ignores every letter, hall and case has exactly today's app.

**Score.**
- The 4.5 brief alone: about 4.44.
- With the Circuit: about **4.58**.
- With the Stop Visitors as well: about **4.60**.

## 2. Laws (binding; each one has a test in §13)

| # | Law |
|---|---|
| L1 | **Overlay.** No separate map, mode or tab. |
| L2 | **Nothing locks anything.** Halls, cases and Team Night never gate a stop, region, game or word. Mail never expires. |
| L3 | **The real world.** Venues, a pronouncer, rivals, nerves and technique. No magic, and no world to save. |
| L4 | **The antagonist is kid-safe.** Dax's "dirty play" is gamesmanship: psych-outs, rumours, blame, one hidden bell. Never violence, threats, cheating that changes a result, insults about anyone's looks, family or worth, or mocking a wrong answer to someone's face. Every wrong he does has a consequence in the story, and his reform is earned on screen. |
| L5 | **Every beat teaches a real technique,** and the letter names the lesson it comes from. |
| L6 | **The story moves on at any placing.** Every rival letter has an *above* and a *below* version. Cases always resolve: hints escalate until the answer is shown. **Team Night** is won by the team (see §8), so the season's ending always lands. The child's own performance changes the lines, never the outcome. |
| L7 | **No child free text.** The Suspect Board, the accusations, the "help Dax?" moment and the closing line are all choices from pre-written lines. |
| L8 | **Written for twelve, not down to eight.** Every line is read aloud and fits one phone screen at 390 px. |
| L9 | **Labelled as fiction.** Every Hive Post page says *"A story about a spelling season. The rivals are made up; the techniques are real."* |
| L10 | **Inkwell's craft** in every case and round: a clock, an impossible thing, suspects with kind secrets, a false solution, a clue in plain sight planted earlier, an escalation, a staged reveal, chapter-end hooks, spooky-but-safe atmosphere, and the mystery closer than you think. The false solution is the story's, never the player's. |
| L11 | **Pay is unchanged.** Halls and Team Night pay as Mock Bee pays (contest coins on merit only). Cases pay 1 coin per right answer. Nothing pays for finishing, mail or visitors. |

|---|---|---|---|

| **The proof** | Case 5 | Reformed Dax spots Dash's spare key and **clears Vesper**: the speller he'd most like beaten. | — |
| **The captain** | Team Night | Vesper declines to captain (*"I've just learned to ask questions. I'm not ready to answer them."*). Kwame proposes Dax. The team doubts him. He wins them over with one idea, learned from losing: **"Speed is nothing. Who takes the word is everything."** | He calls every word to the teammate whose speciality fits (§8.2). The team wins. His last postcard: *"Steady beat. Like you said. — D. (captain, ask anyone)"* |

### B · The mystery: who is "— V."?

**What the child sees.** After every round, a short card in a slanting hand, signed only **— V.** It arrives somewhere impossible:
- on the child's chair before the post;
- inside the locked bell case;
- folded in the dictionary Ratchet "borrowed";
- under the Finish Arch while it was taped off.

Each note is a **riddle with two edges**. It names the child's weakest technique from the bee file's top error type, *and* it predicts something that is about to happen. The notes are intriguing, never cruel.

**The Suspect Board** (Hive Post). Four portraits. Clue cards unlock through the season, and the child may pin any clue under any suspect. The board is never scored. Accusing opens after Round IX.

| Suspect | Why they look guilty | What clears or convicts them |
|---|---|---|
| **Dax** | Everyone's first guess: he's the villain, and V. knows things. | **Cleared at the turn (VIII).** He shows the child a stack of V. notes *sent to him*: *"Slow down. — V."* … *"They aren't against you. — V."*, every one ignored. *"I thought it was you writing them,"* he says. |
| **Vesper** | A V. name, violet ink, silent, already qualified. | **Cleared in Round VI**: *"Not me. I don't write notes. — Vesper."* It's true. The violet pen in Round III was a gift (she admits it at the final). |
| **Ratchet** | "Borrows" things, and was where the dictionary went. | **Cleared**: Ratchet can't spell. Every one of his programmes has a misprint, and V.'s notes never do. |
| **The Pronouncer, V. Abara** | His initial. A framed programme from decades ago: *"Newest speller: V. Abara, aged 8, placed last."* | **Guilty**, and kind. See the clues below. |

**The planted clues to the Pronouncer** (all shown before the final):
1. **Round I:** the Pronouncer asks every speller *"Say it back for me."* The first V. note says *"Say it back."*
2. **Round II:** the framed programme names "V. Abara, aged 8, placed last".
3. **Case 1:** the Pronouncer corrects the misprinted list by hand. His writing slants left, like V.'s.
4. **Round IV:** the note is inside the locked bell case, and only Committee staff hold case keys. (Bolt's secret is that the lock is broken, which makes this a red herring for "anyone" and keeps it fair.)
5. **Round VII:** V.'s note predicted the unheard word. Only the person who sets the list knows the list.
6. **Case 5:** the Pronouncer has a secret he won't tell, even with Vesper accused.

**The accusation (after Round IX, staged).** The child names V. from the board, and a wrong pick gets *"not quite, look again"*. Then the closing letter: *"I placed last, the first time. Somebody wrote to me after every round. I never found out who. So every season, I write to whoever is new. Not to win. To keep them going. — V. Abara, Pronouncer"*

**The sting (after Team Night).** One more note arrives, in a **different** hand, while the Pronouncer is standing beside the child: *"There were always two of us. — V."* It is the Season 2 mystery. Seeds point at Mei of Ravenmere, at Vesper, and at someone not yet met.

### C · Vesper's silence (the suspense)

- **From Round IV:** every invitation names her as already qualified, and her chair on the board is empty. Rumours grow round by round.
- **Case 5:** she is the false solution.
- **The final's blackout:** she asks for the first time in her life: *"Language of origin, please."*
- **Her letter:** she stopped asking the year she won, because asking felt weak. She left the violet pen because *"you were writing in pencil and it was painful to watch."*
- **Team Night:** she spells the Ravenmere tiebreaker *only because Dax calls it to her* (§8).

### D · Pip's rumour mill (the humour)

- One breathless note per round. Each misuses **one** big word, underlined.
- A tap shows the real meaning, read aloud, and adds it to "Pip's words". Never scored.
- In the finale Pip gets one right: *"Vesper is formidable. I looked it up. It means what it sounds like."*

### E · Theo's count (the running gag that becomes the method)

- His tally of questions asked this season: 212 → 640 → 1,204 → 1,977.
- In the blackout final, his questions become everyone's way of spelling.

### F · Kwame's crack and comeback (the drama)

- Two seasons with no public miss, until Round V.
- He vanishes (Case 3) and comes back coached by Ines (Round VI).
- **He is the one who reaches Dax** (Round VIII).

## 6. Season script: the nine rounds

**Key:**

| Label | Means |
|---|---|
| INV | Invitation (the Pronouncer, for the Committee) |
| PROG | Host's programme |
| DRAMA | The round's main event |
| LAUGH | A comic beat |
| SUSPENSE | The tension beat |
| ABOVE / BELOW | The headliner's letter, depending on the child's placing |
| V. | The mystery note |
| DAX | The antagonist beat |
| PIP | Pip's note |
| CLIFF | The cliffhanger |
| CLUE | The clue card for the Suspect Board |

Every piece is ≤ 60 words.

### Round I · The Meadow · the Hive Gates

*Headliners: Pip (8) and Nova (9). Technique: wait for the whole word, then say it back (concept 1, the Champion's Routine).*

- **INV:** "Welcome to the Bee Circuit. Nine rounds. One season. The Grand Marquee at the end. Seated in chairs two and three: Pip, who starts before the word is finished, and Nova, who says every word twice."
- **PROG (Barnaby):** "Please do not feed the bees. The bees are competing."
- **DRAMA:** Pip blurts out the first word before the Pronouncer finishes it and is out in round one, then cheers loudest for everyone.
- **LAUGH:** The Pronouncer: *"Say it back for me."* Pip: *"Say it back for me."* The Pronouncer: *"Not the instructions, Pip."*
- **SUSPENSE:** At the end of the row there is an empty chair with a card reading **RESERVED**. Nobody sits in it.
- **ABOVE (Nova):** "You waited. I saw. My nan's rule: say it twice. Once to hear it, once to check you heard it. — Nova"
- **BELOW (Nova):** "So close. Say it back before your first letter, like the Pronouncer asks. My nan's rule: say it twice. — Nova"
- **V.:** "Say it back. The chair at the end of the row is waiting for someone. So are you. — V."
- **DAX:** A postcard from a venue the child hasn't reached: *"Heard you got through the Meadow. The Sprints are mine. — D. (fastest in round one, ask anyone)"*
- **PIP:** "Dax is *ubiquitous*, which means he has a big head."
- **CLIFF:** The postcard's stamp is from the **Finish Arch**, eight regions away. How is he already there?
- **CLUE:** "Note 1 was on your chair before the post came. It quotes the Pronouncer."

### Round II · The Library · the Grand Archive

*Headliner: Theo (12). Technique: ask for the definition, a sentence and the part of speech.*

- **INV:** "The Grand Archive requests silence, which Theo will not provide. You may ask the Pronouncer for a definition, a sentence, the part of speech. He has to answer."
- **PROG (Sage the Owl):** "We admire Theo's curiosity. We have ordered more chairs. Our press is run by a typesetter who learned English from a parrot."
- **DRAMA:** Theo asks eleven questions about one word and spells it perfectly. The speller two seats down falls asleep.
- **LAUGH:** The Pronouncer, for the eleventh time: "Yes, Theo. It is still a noun."
- **SUSPENSE:** A framed programme from the first circuit, decades ago, reads: *"Newest speller: V. Abara, aged 8, placed last."*
- **ABOVE (Theo):** "You asked three questions and beat me. Imagine eleven. They have to answer. Questions asked this season: 212. — Theo"
- **BELOW (Theo):** "You asked one question. Ask five: definition, sentence, part of speech, origin, alternate pronunciation. Questions asked this season: 212. — Theo"
- **V.:** "You guess instead of asking. Tonight the list will lie to you. Read it twice. — V."
- **DAX:** In the corridor, a fake "tip": *"Separate has an e in the middle, everyone knows."* (The bee file shows the real rule: *sep-A-rate*, "there's a rat in separate".)
- **PIP:** "Theo is *loquacious*, which is a kind of lizard."
- **CLIFF:** Surprise Stop 1: URGENT. *"The list for the Colosseum is back from the press. Every word is wrong."* V. predicted it.
- **CLUE:** "An old programme: V. Abara, aged 8."

### Round III · The Forum · the Colosseum

*Headliner: Rafi (10). Technique: take the Latin root apart.*

- **INV:** "Latin prefixes today. The Colosseum seats several thousand; we have nine. Seated in chair four: Rafi, who takes every root apart before he writes a letter."
- **PROG (Tullia the Tortoise):** "This programme is late. All of my programmes are late."
- **DRAMA:** Rafi traces every word on his palm and is the last speller standing beside the child. His hand is shaking by the end.
- **LAUGH:** Tullia's programme arrives after the round has finished.
- **SUSPENSE:** A violet pen is left on the child's chair. Violet is Vesper's ink.
- **ABOVE (Rafi):** "You beat me on *circumference*. *Circum* means around. Split it before you spell it. Draw the next word small on your palm. — Rafi"
- **BELOW (Rafi):** "*Circum* means around, so *circumference* is the line around. Split it first. Try my palm trick. — Rafi"
- **V.:** "Every long word is a short word wearing a coat. When the bell goes missing, look for the one who's scared of thunder. — V."
- **DAX:** In Case 1 he told everyone Theo did it. *"Who else hangs around a printing press?"* He shrugs off being wrong.
- **PIP:** "Rafi is *meticulous*, which is a type of fish."
- **CLIFF:** The Storm invitation says Vesper is "already qualified for the Grand Marquee". The RESERVED chair now has a name.
- **CLUE:** "A violet pen. Vesper writes in violet."

### Round IV · The Storm · the Eye of the Storm

*Headliner: Mira (12). Technique: ask for the origin. In Greek, an f sound is spelled ph, a k sound ch, and an i sound y.*

- **INV:** "Today's weather: Greek, with a strong chance of ph. Seated in chair one: Mira, who asks every word where it was born."
- **PROG (Bolt the Ram):** "Expect thunder, and a ringing in your ears."
- **DRAMA:** Thunder hits mid-word. Mira doesn't flinch. She asks the origin, hears "Greek", and smiles.
- **LAUGH:** Mira asks for the origin of the word "the". The Pronouncer answers, at length.
- **SUSPENSE:** That night, before Kwame's round, the Committee bell vanishes from its locked case. That is Surprise Stop 2 (§7). V. called it in Round III.
- **ABOVE (Mira):** "You asked for the origin twice. Do it every time. Greek words travel with their luggage: ph, ch, y. — Mira"
- **BELOW (Mira):** "*Phantom* with an f! Ask where it came from and the word tells you its rules. — Mira"
- **V.** (on the child's chair, before the post): "Words keep their passports. Check them. Someone in this hall is about to lose more than a round. — V."
- **DAX:** In Case 2, straight after this round, he is caught hiding the spare bell. The Pronouncer, quietly: *"First warning, Dax."*
- **PIP:** "Mira is *clairvoyant*, which means she has clear skin."
- **CLIFF:** V. said someone would lose more than a round. Who?
- **CLUE** (unlocked by Case 2): "A note inside a locked case. Only Committee staff hold the keys."

### Round V · The Roots · the Root Throne

*Headliner: Kwame (14). Technique: build the word from its parts.*

- **INV:** "Kwame has not misspelled a word in public in two seasons. Seated beside him: you."
- **PROG (Hazel the Hedgehog):** "Spectators are asked not to roll up into balls during sudden death."
- **DRAMA:** Kwame misspells a French-derived word. The hall goes silent. Hands still behind his back, he walks out.
- **DAX (the line crossed):** In the corridor, loud: *"Told you. Nobody's perfect. Two seasons, gone in one word."* Nobody laughs. Rafi turns his back on him.
- **LAUGH:** Hazel rolls into a ball during sudden death. (It eases the hall *before* the crack, not after.)
- **SUSPENSE:** V. predicted it.

## 7. The Surprise Stops (Inkwell-style cases)

**Format (5–8 minutes per case):**
1. Cold open: two panels and a clock on screen.
2. Scene: three things to tap, one of them the planted clue.
3. Puzzle: 8–12 items on an existing engine, at the child's band, through `pick()`.
4. An escalation card halfway through.
5. Accusation: three choices. A wrong pick gets *"not quite, look again"* and the right clue glows.
6. Staged reveal.
7. A last chill.

### 8.2 The Team Bee mechanic (new `mockbee.js` team mode)

- **Teams.** The Circuit is the child plus four teammates. Ravenmere has five spellers. Teammates are seated by band: the 8–10 band gets Nova, Rafi, Mira and Dax; the 11–15 band gets Ines, Kwame, Theo and Vesper (with Dax as captain).
- **Each turn:**
  1. The word is pronounced.
  2. **The child plays the captain's caller**: from the definition and the "Ask the Pronouncer" questions, pick the language of origin, or pick "nobody's heard it", "needs questions" or "steady beat".
  3. A correct call sends the word to the right specialist, who spells it at their skill (simulated). A wrong call sends it to the wrong teammate, whose simulated chance falls.
  4. Every third word is the child's own to spell (steady beat).
- **What it teaches.** This is the season's whole lesson in one mechanic: *recognise where a word comes from and you know who can spell it.* The bee file after Team Night shows the child's origin-calling accuracy.
- **Scoring.** A team point per word. Ravenmere's spellers are simulated at the top of the child's band.
- **The narrative win (L6).** The team always wins narrowly. Ravenmere's simulated misses are seeded so the Circuit's last words decide it, and the tiebreaker goes to Vesper. The child's play changes the margin, the lines and the stamp, never the outcome. Contest coins pay **only** on the child's own spelled words and correct calls (L11), so a random tapper still earns 0.
- **Replays.** Team Night can be replayed against Ravenmere's "second team" for a better margin. The story text doesn't repeat; a replay gets a short programme.
