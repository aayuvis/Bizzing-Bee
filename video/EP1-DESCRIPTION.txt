Everybody will tell you the first National Spelling Bee was 1925. It wasn't.

In June 1908, at the Hippodrome Theater in Cleveland, the National Education Association ran the first national spelling contest in America — seventeen years earlier. The individual champion was Marie C. Bolden, a thirteen-year-old Black student and the daughter of a mail carrier, and she was the only speller on her team to finish with no errors at all. Members of the all-white New Orleans team had objected to competing against her. Her gold medal has never been found.

This film is the story of that contest, and of the four bees that followed from 1925 to 1928 — who won them, what words they won on, and what became of those children afterwards.

A NOTE ON HOW THIS WAS MADE
The narration in this film is AI-generated, and so is the artwork — the theatre, the schoolhouse, the pressroom, and the cartoon avatars of the champions. The photographs are the exception: every photograph you see is a genuine archive image from the Library of Congress or the Smithsonian, and none of them has been altered or generated.

The champions appear as cartoon avatars from the Bizzing Bee app, drawn in the same style as the rest of that collection. They are characters inspired by real children, not claimed likenesses, and none is drawn from a specific photograph. Nothing generated in this film is made to look like a photograph, so you can always tell which images are the historical record and which are illustration.
