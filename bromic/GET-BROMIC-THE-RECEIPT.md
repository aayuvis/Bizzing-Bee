# GET BROMIC — The Receipt
### Second format: the price stack, with the ending inverted

Everyone over forty knows the cadence: a stack of itemised prices, then a final line that money can't buy.
**Bromic's version inverts the ending** — because the whole argument is that the thing which unlocks the
backyard *is* buyable, and it is the cheapest line on the list.

That inversion is what makes it a Bromic joke rather than a borrowed one. It is also the most on-strategy
idea in the platform: it states the argument as arithmetic, in public, in the customer's own numbers.

## Why it earns its place

The films carry the **fame**; the receipts carry the **argument**. That is a healthier platform than films
alone, and it fixes the one real weakness of the before/after device — every execution needs a shoot.
A receipt is pure typography: near-zero cost, produced in a day, endlessly renewable, and it re-skins
instantly for a homeowner, a builder or an operator by swapping the line items. It is also the most
screenshot-able asset in the platform and the easiest thing a dealer can put on a counter card.

> **Craft note.** Set these as real typography, never as AI-generated images — image models render text
> unreliably, and this format is nothing but text and numbers.

## Legal — read before this goes anywhere

**Never use the word that ends the original.** It is a registered Mastercard trademark and among the most
aggressively protected properties in advertising.

Do not reproduce their sign-off construction, their voiceover cadence, their type treatment, or their card.
The itemised price stack itself is a generic device used widely and is not theirs — the protected assets are
the wording and the lockup.

The inversion helps here as well as creatively: we are not claiming anything is unbuyable, we are claiming
the opposite. Still, have counsel clear the final wording before production. This is the one idea in the
deck where the legal review is not a formality.

All figures above are illustrative and must be replaced with verified pricing before anything runs.
