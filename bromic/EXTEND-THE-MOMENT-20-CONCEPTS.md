## The 20

---

### 1. THE MUSHROOM
`BOTH` `:30` `REMODEL` `ASSET` ✅ `FAME` `R-LO`

An $89 hardware-store mushroom heater standing dead centre in a $70,000 alfresco build like
a traffic cone. Three adults pressed into its 1.2-metre radius, shoulder to shoulder, while
the outdoor kitchen, the twelve-seat table and the built-in lounge sit empty around them.

> "Seventy thousand dollars of outdoor kitchen. Heated by an eighty-nine dollar mushroom."

*Bunnings in AUS, Home Depot in US — the object is identical in both and instantly readable.
Best single concept in the bank: it does the price reframe and the gag in one frame.*

---

### 2. THE PIZZA OVEN
`BOTH` `:30` `ADDON` `SUNKCOST` ✅ `FAME` `R-LO`

A $9,000 wood-fired oven. He fires it, tends it, peels the pizza — beautiful. Everyone takes
a slice and goes inside. He stands alone in the dark in a puffer, holding the peel, next to
the most expensive appliance he has ever bought.

> "Four times a year. Extend the Moment."

*Pizza ovens are an epidemic in both markets. Painfully recognisable, zero cultural
translation needed.*

---

### 3. THE SNOWBIRDS / THE QUEENSLAND RUN
`TWIN` `:30` `REMODEL` `ASSET` ✅ `FAME` `R-LO`

A retired couple's entire plan was the terrace. Instead, every year, they pack the car and
drive five hours north for five months.

> **US VO:** "The Wilsons solved their outdoor heating problem. It cost four hundred
> thousand dollars." *(cut to: a condo in Scottsdale)*
>
> **AUS VO:** "The Wilsons solved their outdoor heating problem. It cost four hundred
> thousand dollars." *(cut to: a unit on the Sunshine Coast)*

*Snowbirds in the US, grey nomads / the winter run to QLD in AUS — same behaviour, same
joke, swap the destination plate. The most extreme possible statement of the price reframe,
and it lands hardest on the 55–70 end of the target.*

---

### 4. THE RENO SHOW REVEAL
`TWIN` `:30` `REMODEL` `ASSET` ✅ `FAME` `R-MED`

Renovation-show reveal. Judges walk through the house — glowing. They slide open the doors
to the alfresco/terrace, step out, and within four seconds silently step back in.

> **Judge:** "Alfresco. Big tick." *(beat)* "Can we go back inside?"

Scores collapse.

*`AUS`: The Block treatment. `US`: HGTV / Property Brothers reveal treatment. Both markets
have a decade of shared visual grammar for this — the walk-through, the hands over the eyes,
the scorecard. `R-MED` only because it needs parody handled cleanly, not imitation of a
specific show's branding.*

---

### 5. THE COASTAL BREEZE
`BOTH` `:30` `NEWBUILD` `ASSET` ✅ `CONSIDER` `R-LO`

The beach house. Flawless. Every evening at five past five, the wind arrives and the deck
empties in under a minute — plates lifted, napkins gone, everyone in. Every single night.
Time-lapsed across a week so it becomes a rhythm.

> "Built for the view. Emptied by the breeze."

*The only concept that dramatises Bromic's actual technical differentiator — wind-resistant
infrared, which Infratech literally cannot answer. `AUS`: Byron, Mornington, Palm Beach.
`US`: Cape Cod, the Hamptons, coastal California. Put this one in front of architects.*

---

### 6. THE FIRE PIT ROTISSERIE
`BOTH` `:15` `SOCIAL` `ADDON` `ASSET` ✅ `FAME` `R-LO`

Six people around a fire pit, faces roasting, backs frozen, rotating 180° in silent
synchronisation every ninety seconds. Locked-off wide. Pure choreography, no dialogue.

> "The outdoor sofa cost eleven thousand dollars. Everyone is sitting on a log."

*Note the caption does the work — it converts a cold gag into an asset gag. Without that
line this is a mushroom-heater ad.*

---

### 7. THE BLANKET BASKET
`BOTH` `STATIC` `:15` `REMODEL` `ASSET` ✅ `FAME` `R-LO`

Slow reverent push-in on the wicker basket of throws by the back door, lit like a war
memorial.

> "Erected in memory of the outdoor season."

*This object exists in every single home in the target. Cheapest concept in the bank and
one of the most likely to be screenshotted.*

---

### 8. THE GROUP CHAT
`BOTH` `SOCIAL` `:15` `REMODEL` `STATUS` ✅ `FAME` `R-LO`

Texts on screen, 9:16, native format:

> **"Still on for tonight? 🎉"**
> **"Yes! Though it's meant to drop to 9."**
> **"…indoors?"**
> **"Indoors 👍"**

Then the same four messages, four times, four different dates — the entire scroll history of
a terrace nobody has used since March.

*Cheapest possible production, most native to feed, works identically in both markets
(swap °C for °F). Ideal always-on filler between film flights.*

---

### 9. THE OPEN HOME / THE LISTING PHOTO
`TWIN` `:30` `NEWBUILD` `ASSET` ✅ `CONSIDER` `R-LO`

An agent stages the outdoor kitchen for the listing shoot — cushions plumped, cheeseboard,
two glasses of prosecco nobody will drink. The shutter clicks. Everyone instantly sprints
inside. Cut to the finished listing.

> **Listing copy on screen:** "Seamless indoor-outdoor living."

*`US`: Zillow listing. `AUS`: realestate.com.au and the Saturday open home. Aims squarely at
people buying a new home — a segment your brief named and that most heater marketing
completely ignores.*

---

### 10. IT'S NOT THAT COLD / SHE'LL BE RIGHT
`TWIN` `:15` `SOCIAL` `ADDON` `COLD` ⚠️ `FAME` `R-LO`

The dad, alone at his own party, in a beanie, on the good outdoor furniture, insisting.

> **US:** "It's actually really nice out here." *(nobody comes)*
>
> **AUS:** "She'll be right." *(nobody comes)*

Slow pull back to reveal fourteen people inside, through glass, not looking at him.

⚠️ *Cold-axis — fix in the turn: cut to the same shot with the heat on and all fourteen
outside, and land it on the furniture, not the temperature. Universally recognisable dad
energy in both markets; the idiom is the only thing that changes.*

---

### 11. MELBOURNE (FOUR SEASONS IN ONE DINNER)
`AUS` `:30` `REMODEL` `COLD` ⚠️ `FAME` `R-LO`

One dinner party. Four complete weather cycles. Guests add and shed layers in a rising
rhythm — sunglasses, then coats, then shirtsleeves, then blankets — without ever
acknowledging it or pausing the conversation.

> "Melbourne. Extend the Moment."

*Melbourne's self-mythology about its weather is the single most reliable joke in Australian
advertising. `US` transplant works for Denver, Chicago and San Francisco with a city card
swap.*

---

### 12. ALFRESCO (N.)
`AUS` `STATIC` `REMODEL` `ASSET` ✅ `FAME` `R-LO`

Immaculate empty alfresco area, autumn light, set in Bromic's existing type.

> **Alfresco** *(n.)* — Italian: "in the open air."
> Australian: "the bit near the barbecue that nobody uses after May."

*`AUS`-native — "alfresco" is a specific, load-bearing word in Australian home-building that
does not carry the same meaning in the US. US twin exists as "Pergola (n.): a structure for
looking at."*

---

### 13. LABOR DAY / EASTER — THE CLOSING CEREMONY
`TWIN` `:30` `REMODEL` `ASSET` ✅ `FAME` `R-LO`

A family solemnly closes the pool, folds the umbrellas, stacks the cushions and wheels the
grill into the garage. Shot like a funeral — slow, wordless, one family member visibly
emotional.

> **US VO:** "The Petersons observe the end of summer. It is seventy-eight degrees."
>
> **AUS VO:** "The Petersons observe the end of summer. It is twenty-six degrees."

*`US`: Labor Day is a hard cultural full-stop on outdoor life. `AUS`: Easter plays the same
role. The absurdity — that the weather is still perfect — is the joke, and it's the exact
argument for buying.*

---

### 14. THE BIG GAME / GRAND FINAL
`TWIN` `:30` `ADDON` `ASSET` ✅ `FAME` `R-LO`

Twenty-two people crammed into a living room around a 55-inch screen. Push through the glass
doors behind them to reveal the outdoor lounge, the bar, and its own 85-inch outdoor
television — dark, dry under the roof, completely empty.

> "The outdoor TV cost six thousand dollars. Everyone is watching the small one."

*`US`: Super Bowl Sunday, February. `AUS`: the footy finals / Grand Final, September.
Identical shot, opposite hemisphere, opposite month — the same film ships to both markets
with one supered date card.*

### 15. THE HOUSEWARMING
`BOTH` `:30` `NEWBUILD` `STATUS` ✅ `FAME` `R-LO`

The invitation reads **"Outdoor party — dress warm!"** Twenty adults arrive in ski jackets,
beanies and technical fleece, holding champagne, standing in an award-winning designer
garden looking like a Himalayan base camp. Nobody mentions it.

> "You should not need equipment to attend a dinner party."

*Status axis — this one is about the humiliation of a beautiful thing not working, which is
the emotional core of the whole platform.*

---

### 16. GRAND DESIGNS — THE YEAR AFTER
`BOTH` `:30` `TRADE` `ASSET` ✅ `CONSIDER` `R-MED`

The presenter returns twelve months on for the follow-up. The house is extraordinary.
They walk the owners through it, delighted. Then:

> "And how are you finding the terrace?"

A very, very long silence. The owners look at each other.

*Grand Designs is enormous in AUS and well-known in the US via streaming. Aimed at the trade
tier — this is the one that makes an architect uncomfortable in a useful way, because the
unused terrace is *their* failure, not the client's.*

---

### 17. THE SPECIFICATION
`BOTH` `:30` `TRADE` `ASSET` ✅ `CONSIDER` `R-LO`

An architect presents a gorgeous render of an outdoor room. Golden light, people, wine, the
works. The client studies it, then points at it.

> **Client:** "And in July?" *(AUS)* / "And in November?" *(US)*

The architect opens their mouth. Nothing comes out. Hold on it.

> "Specify the season. Not just the space."

*The trade-tier workhorse. LinkedIn, trade press, rep leave-behind. Keeps the endline,
shifts the victim to the specifier's competence — the only currency that moves that
audience.*

---

### 18. TWO HOURS
`BOTH` `:15` `STATIC` `REMODEL` `ASSET` ✅ `CONSIDER` `R-LO`

Locked-off time-lapse of one terrace. 4:00pm — full, loud, twelve people. 6:00pm — six.
7:00pm — one chair pushed out, a half-finished drink, nobody.

> "Your terrace has an opening time and a closing time. You never agreed to either."

*Quietly the most persuasive concept in the bank for someone actually considering the
purchase — it's an argument disguised as a joke. Pairs well as the `CONSIDER` retargeting
asset behind the funnier fame films.*

---

### 19. THE KIDS' TABLE
`US` `:30` `REMODEL` `ASSET` ✅ `FAME` `R-HI`

Thanksgiving. The kids' table has been put outside. Four children in winter coats, plates of
turkey going cold, staring through the glass at the warm, loud adults inside. One child
maintains unbroken eye contact with camera.

> "There is a better solution."

*`US`-native — Thanksgiving overflow seating is a universally understood domestic crime.
`R-HI`: dark, and needs a warm turn (heat on, the table brought back into the party) or it
reads as mean. Test before committing. AUS has no clean equivalent.*

---

### 20. THE DECK
`BOTH` `:15` `SOCIAL` `ADDON` `SUNKCOST` ✅ `FAME` `R-LO`

A man oils, sweeps and polishes a deck the way other men polish a car — devoted, unhurried,
loving. He steps back. He admires it. He goes inside. Nobody has sat on it in seven months.

> "Beautifully maintained. Entirely unused."

*The most Australian and most American thing in the bank at the same time — deck pride is
identical in both countries.*

## Note on the hemisphere flip

The seasons invert, so the media calendars do too — and that's an advantage, not a problem.
The same production runs **Aug–Nov in the US**, then **Feb–May in Australia**, six months
later, off one shoot. Concepts 13 and 14 are built specifically to exploit this: identical
film, one supered card, opposite ends of the year.
