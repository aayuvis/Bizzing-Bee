01 · The argument beneath the promise

Keep the promise.
Add the argument.

Extend the Moment is the right promise and it stays. It is the brand’s equity, it is what the product genuinely delivers, and it is where every one of these films resolves to. Nothing here replaces it.

What a promise cannot do on its own is create urgency. It asks someone to want more of something already pleasant — a soft ask against a $4,000 installed line item, and the reason this category gets deferred to next year, every year. So the campaign supplies the other half: not a new promise, an argument underneath the one that already works.

You didn't spend eighty thousand dollars on a backyard
to look at it through glass.

That argument does the commercial work. It moves the purchase out of “heater” — a commodity, price-shopped against an $89 mushroom from the hardware store — and into “protecting the investment you already made”, where the comparison set is the pergola, the outdoor kitchen and the pool, and $4,000 is a rounding error. Extend the Moment tells them what they get. This tells them what it is costing them not to.

It is also the only version of this argument that works on an architect. They don't want to hear that heat is nice. They want to hear that an unheated outdoor room is unused square footage they designed — and will be blamed for.

02 · The discipline filter — the rule the platform lives or dies on

Passes — a Bromic joke

Jokes about the wasted asset

The $80k backyard sitting like a wasteland. The pizza oven used four times. The six-thousand-dollar outdoor TV nobody watches.

Only a premium, built-in, specified brand can tell these. They get the laugh and justify the premium in the same breath.

Fails — anyone's joke

Jokes about being cold

Chattering teeth. Blankets. Don't sleep inside a horse.

Funny, but free for every competitor — and they position the product as a remedy for discomfort, which is a commodity job at a commodity price. They drag Bromic down-market.

The test: if the joke would still work for an $89 hardware-store heater, kill it or rewrite the caption onto the asset. Applied to the seed ideas — wasteland passes, the horse needs surgery. It survives below as Don't Do It, Gary, built on implication rather than gore, which also solves the IP and animal-welfare problems at the same time.

06 · Second format

The Receipt.

Everyone over forty knows the cadence: a stack of itemised prices, then a final line that money can't buy. Bromic's version inverts the ending — because the whole argument is that the thing which unlocks the backyard is buyable, and it is the cheapest line on the list.

That inversion is what makes it a Bromic joke rather than a borrowed one. It is also the single most on-strategy idea in the platform: it states the argument as arithmetic, in public, in the customer's own numbers.

Run each one — the lines land in sequence, as they would in a film or a scroll-stop

Why this format earns its place

The films carry the fame; the receipts carry the argument. That is a healthier platform than films alone — and it fixes the one real weakness of the before/after device, which is that every execution needs a shoot. A receipt is pure typography: near-zero cost, produced in a day, endlessly renewable, and it re-skins instantly for a homeowner, a builder or an operator by swapping the line items.

It is also the most screenshot-able thing in the platform, and the easiest asset a dealer can put on a counter card.

Legal — read before this goes anywhere

Never use the word that ends the original. It is a registered Mastercard trademark and among the most aggressively protected properties in advertising.

Do not reproduce their sign-off construction, their voiceover cadence, their type treatment, or their card. The itemised price stack itself is a generic device used widely and is not theirs — the protected assets are the wording and the lockup.

The inversion helps here as well as creatively: we are not claiming anything is unbuyable, we are claiming the opposite. Still, have counsel clear the final wording before production. This is the one idea in the deck where the legal review is not a formality.

The hemisphere advantage

Seasons invert, so the media calendars do too — and that's an asset. The same production runs Aug–Nov in the US, then Feb–May in Australia, six months later, off one shoot. Two concepts are built specifically to exploit it: identical film, one supered card, opposite ends of the year.

08 · Tier 2 — builders and commercial end users

Same platform.
Different currency.

The consumer work sells the cost of an unused backyard. Neither of these audiences cares about that. A builder's currency is reputation, callbacks and referral; an operator's is covers, reviews and labour. Same endline, same art direction, same deadpan — the victim of the joke changes.

Register discipline: these run Deadpan or Dry only. Never Absurd, never Dark. A restaurateur signing off forty units does not want jokes about being cold, and a builder does not want to be the punchline in front of a client. The humour here is recognition, not gags — every one of these should make the audience think that has happened to me, not laugh out loud.

10 · Who this is for

Two tiers. Never one campaign.

Do not aim at homeowners and architects simultaneously. They want opposite things and the compromise satisfies neither.

Tier 1 — Homeowners, 40–70

Segments
Remodelling · adding on · buying or building new
Job
Fame. Be the name they say at the showroom counter.
Targeting
Not "patio heater" intent — that's a small, bottom-funnel, price-shopping pool. Buy the moment before the category occurs to them: renovation and custom-build intent, recently-moved high-value homes, outdoor kitchen and pool audiences, premium appliance affinities, Houzz and AD readership, and lookalikes off the dealer CRM.
Channels
Meta feed + Reels (9:16 first), YouTube, and CTV — which this demo over-indexes on and where a 30-second film with production values actually gets watched. Pinterest and Houzz for statics.
Geography
Concentrate. Real season plus real money: Northeast, Mid-Atlantic, Chicago, Mountain, PNW, coastal California. AUS: Melbourne, Sydney, Adelaide, coastal NSW/VIC.
Timing
Aug–Nov (US) / Feb–May (AUS) is the entire consumer year.

Tier 2 — Architects, designers, builders, commercial

Job
Specification. Get onto the drawing.
Message
Same endline and art direction, none of the joke: coverage, mounting and recess detail, wind performance, marine grade, warranty, CEU.
Channels
LinkedIn, trade press, ArchDaily, showroom and dealer co-op, rep visits.
Timing
Jan–Mar, when renovation planning and spec activity happen.
Bridge
Plate 06 is the only humorous B2B asset — its victim is the builder's reputation, the one currency that moves that audience.
The channel that decides everything is the dealer. This category closes at a counter, by a person. Budget for co-branded cutdowns, in-store statics, a showroom badge, and a one-page sales sheet built on the reframe — the heater is 4% of the build and the difference between eleven months and two.

11 · How this gets judged

Measure fame, not clicks

Primary
Branded search volume for "Bromic" — cleanest, fastest, cheapest read. Baseline it now.
Primary
Unaided and aided awareness in target, pre/post.
Primary
One tracked field in the dealer portal: "customer asked for us by name." Nearly free, and it proves the entire thesis.
Causal
Matched-market geo holdout — six DMAs live, six held out, read dealer sell-through. The only credible ROI evidence for a brand play, and what protects the year-two budget.
Diagnostic
3s views, thruplay, completion. Watch them; never optimise to them.
Not a KPI
CTR, CPC, CPL on the brand films. Sign this before launch.

This is how campaigns like this die: a dashboard shows 0.4% CTR in week three, someone panics, the money moves to retargeting. That risk is managed with a signed document, not optimism. Split roughly 60 brand / 40 activation in year one — the funny work manufactures demand and something has to be standing there to harvest it.

12 · Red team

What could break it

High
Attribution failure. Brand in first 3s and endline, product in the turn, fixed audio mnemonic, one VO voice across the library.
High
Alienating specifiers. Hold the craft at luxury standard — cinema lensing, real houses, real light. The joke lives in the writing, never the production value. Pre-show to 8–10 partner firms and let them feel consulted.
Medium
Down-market drift. The filter, enforced every round.
Medium
IP. Never name the film, recreate a shot, or touch a score. No visible animal harm. Legal-review the Gary film before production.
Medium
Competitive counter. Expect a price and made-locally response. Bake one product truth into each turn so the humour still carries a reason to believe.
Medium
Commercial tonal mismatch. A restaurateur buying forty units doesn't want jokes about being cold. Trade tier runs straight.
