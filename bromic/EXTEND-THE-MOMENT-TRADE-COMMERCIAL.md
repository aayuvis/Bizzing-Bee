# EXTEND THE MOMENT — Tier 2
### Builders and commercial end users — full campaign briefs

The consumer work sells the cost of an unused backyard. Neither of these audiences cares about that.
A builder's currency is **reputation, callbacks and referral**; an operator's is **covers, reviews and
labour**. Same endline, same art direction, same deadpan — the victim of the joke changes.

> **Register discipline:** these run **Deadpan or Dry only**. Never Absurd, never Dark. A restaurateur
> signing off forty units does not want jokes about being cold, and a builder does not want to be the
> punchline in front of a client. The humour here is recognition, not gags.

## Builders, architects and remodellers

### B1. The Punch List
`BOTH` · `ASSET` · `BUILDER` · `CONSIDER` · `R-LO`

> **The last item on every punch list is the one you didn’t quote for.**  
> Extend the Moment.

**The scene** — Final walkthrough. The client works down a clipboard ticking off a flawless build — then writes one more line at the bottom: “Can we use it in winter?” Hold on the builder’s face.

**Insight** — Every builder has been ambushed at handover by a question they should have answered at framing.

**The commercial argument** — An unheated outdoor room is an unclosed job. It turns a finished build into an open conversation, and open conversations cost the builder time they cannot bill.

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | :30 film · :15 · static |
| Audience | Custom home builders, remodellers, project managers |
| Channel | LinkedIn, trade press, rep leave-behind, builder association newsletters. |
| Call to action | Get the spec kit — mounting, recess and rough-in details. |
| Measured on | Spec-kit downloads; rep meetings booked. |

**Production note** — Cast a real builder if possible — this audience detects an actor instantly. Shoot on a genuine near-complete site.

### B2. The Referral That Never Came
`BOTH` · `ASSET` · `BUILDER` · `FAME` · `R-LO`

> **“Who built yours?” … “We love it. We’re inside a lot.”**  
> Extend the Moment.

**The scene** — Two neighbours over a fence. The question every builder’s business depends on gets asked — and the answer arrives one beat too late, with one qualifier too many. The referral dies in the pause.

**Insight** — Custom builders live almost entirely on word of mouth. A lukewarm answer is worse than no answer.

**The commercial argument** — The unused terrace does not generate a complaint — it generates a hesitation. Hesitation is the thing that quietly costs a builder their next three jobs.

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | :30 film · :15 |
| Audience | Custom home builders, design-build firms |
| Channel | LinkedIn, trade press, dealer co-op, builder events. |
| Call to action | Book a rep walkthrough on your next build. |
| Measured on | Unaided builder awareness; rep meetings booked. |

**Production note** — The whole film rests on one pause. Cast for timing, not looks. Shoot at magic hour so the unused terrace behind them reads clearly.

### B3. The Change Order
`BOTH` · `ASSET` · `BUILDER` · `CONSIDER` · `R-LO`

> **Rough it in now. Or cut it open later.**  
> Extend the Moment.

**The scene** — A tradesperson cuts a neat rectangular hole into a beautiful finished cedar-lined outdoor ceiling to run a circuit. Sawdust falls onto new stone. The homeowner watches, arms folded.

**Insight** — Retrofit is the single most expensive way to add outdoor heat — and the most common, because nobody roughed in the circuit.

**The commercial argument** — The most practical, most useful thing this platform can say to a builder: pre-wire during build. It costs the client almost nothing at framing and it is a genuine, defensible upsell the builder can put on the quote today.

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | :30 film · :15 · static · one-page sell sheet |
| Audience | Builders, electricians, project managers |
| Channel | LinkedIn, trade press, CEU content, rep leave-behind, dealer counter card. |
| Call to action | Download the rough-in guide. |
| Measured on | Rough-in guide downloads; pre-wire attach rate reported by dealers. |

**Production note** — Highest-conversion asset in the trade tier because it hands the builder money. Get the electrical detail exactly right — this audience will notice a wrong gauge or box.

### B4. The Portfolio Shot
`BOTH` · `ASSET` · `BUILDER` · `FAME` · `R-LO`

> **“Can we get one with people in it?”**  
> Extend the Moment.

**The scene** — A photographer shoots the hero image for the builder’s portfolio. The terrace is magnificent and completely empty. The builder asks for one with people in it. Nobody moves. Long beat.

**Insight** — The portfolio is the builder’s entire marketing department. Empty hero shots are the industry’s open secret.

**The commercial argument** — An outdoor room that cannot be photographed in use is an outdoor room that cannot sell the next job. This reframes heat as a marketing asset for the builder, not a comfort feature for the client.

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | :30 film · :15 · statics |
| Audience | Custom builders, design-build firms, architects |
| Channel | LinkedIn, Instagram to trade, ArchDaily, trade press. |
| Call to action | See the reference builds. |
| Measured on | Reference-build page visits; trade social engagement. |

**Production note** — Ends on the photographer lowering the camera. Very cheap to shoot — one location, three cast. Pairs naturally with a reference-build content series.

### B5. Six Hundred Square Feet
`BOTH` · `ASSET` · `BUILDER` · `CONSIDER` · `R-LO`

> **You sold them 4,200 square feet. You built them 4,800 — for five months a year.**  
> Extend the Moment.

**The scene** — A builder paces out a finished covered terrace at dusk, plans under one arm, gesturing across a beautiful empty space with furniture still wrapped in plastic.

**Insight** — Builders sell square footage. An unheated outdoor room is square footage that does not count for most of the year.

**The commercial argument** — The strongest single argument in the trade tier, because it speaks the unit builders actually price in. Heat is not a comfort upgrade — it is the cheapest square footage on the entire job.

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | :30 film · :15 · static · calculator tool |
| Audience | Builders, architects, developers |
| Channel | LinkedIn, trade press, CEU, rep leave-behind, dealer sell sheet. |
| Call to action | Run the numbers on your next build. |
| Measured on | Calculator use; attach rate on new builds. |

**Production note** — Support it with a real cost-per-square-foot comparison the rep can leave behind. The film opens the door; the arithmetic closes it.

## Commercial end users — restaurants, hotels, bars, venues

### C1. Forty Covers
`BOTH` · `ASSET` · `COMMERCIAL` · `CONSIDER` · `R-LO`

> **Forty covers. Closed for the season.**  
> Extend the Moment.

**The scene** — A beautifully set restaurant terrace, roped off, cold and dark. Camera tracks past it to the packed golden interior — and the queue of people waiting near the door.

**Insight** — Every hospitality operator knows their cover count and their revenue per cover to the dollar.

**The commercial argument** — The sharpest commercial argument in the platform: this is not a comfort purchase, it is capacity. Forty covers × average spend × the nights you currently cannot sell them. The payback period is short enough to put on a single slide.

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | :30 film · :15 · static · ROI one-pager |
| Audience | Restaurant, bar and hotel owners and operators |
| Channel | Trade press, LinkedIn, hospitality trade shows, distributor and rep channels. |
| Call to action | Get the payback calculation for your venue. |
| Measured on | ROI calculator completions; commercial quote requests. |

**Production note** — Lead the commercial tier with this. The supered arithmetic at the end does more work than the film — keep it accurate and conservative.

### C2. The Two-Star Review
`BOTH` · `ASSET` · `COMMERCIAL` · `FAME` · `R-MED`

> **Your terrace is writing your reviews.**  
> Extend the Moment.

**The scene** — An owner alone at the bar after close, lit by a tablet. On it: “Beautiful food. Sat outside. Couldn’t feel my hands. ★★”

**Insight** — Operators are haunted by reviews in a way no other audience is, and a cold terrace generates them reliably every shoulder season.

**The commercial argument** — The cost of an unheated terrace is not just the empty seats — it is the seats you did fill, badly, and the permanent public record they leave behind.

### C3. The Meteorologist
`BOTH` · `ASSET` · `COMMERCIAL` · `CONSIDER` · `R-LO`

> **Your booking calendar should not have a meteorologist.**  
> Extend the Moment.

**The scene** — Four in the afternoon. An events manager stands in a beautiful empty courtyard set for sixty, refreshing a weather app, then making the call that cancels the booking. Repeated across a season.

**Insight** — Weather-driven cancellation is a line item every venue absorbs silently and nobody has ever marketed against.

**The commercial argument** — Revenue certainty, not comfort. A venue that can guarantee its outdoor space can sell it in advance — and stop refunding it.

| Call to action | Get the venue specification guide. |

**Production note** — Functions and weddings are the highest-value commercial sub-segment and the most weather-exposed. Consider a dedicated cut for that trade.

### C4. The Heater Graveyard
`BOTH` · `SUNKCOST` · `COMMERCIAL` · `CONSIDER` · `R-LO`

> **Ten years of solving it. Ten thousand dollars of not solving it.**  
> Extend the Moment.

**The scene** — A back-of-house storeroom crammed with fifteen mismatched, dented, rusting mushroom heaters and gas bottles — the accumulated archaeology of a decade of cheap fixes. Camera tracks the row like a museum exhibit.

**Insight** — Every venue has this room. Nobody has ever added up what is in it.

**The commercial argument** — Total cost of ownership — the argument that wins procurement conversations. Ten years of replacing disposable heaters costs more than installing permanent ones once, before you count the gas, the storage and the labour.

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | :30 film · :15 · static · TCO one-pager |
| Audience | Operators, facilities and procurement managers, hotel groups |
| Channel | Trade press, LinkedIn, procurement and facilities channels, distributor reps. |
| Call to action | Get the ten-year cost comparison. |
| Measured on | TCO one-pager downloads; multi-site enquiries. |

**Production note** — Source a genuinely grim real storeroom — do not build it. The comedy is entirely in it being real. Strongest asset for multi-site groups.

### C5. Four Hours a Week
`BOTH` · `ASSET` · `COMMERCIAL` · `CONSIDER` · `R-LO`

> **Four hours a week. Moving gas bottles.**  
> Extend the Moment.

**The scene** — A server in uniform wheels two heavy propane bottles across a wet courtyard in the rain at the start of a shift. And at the end. And the next day. Timestamped, repeating.

**Insight** — Portable heaters are not a product decision, they are a labour decision — and hospitality labour is the industry’s scarcest resource.

**The commercial argument** — Every hour spent handling bottles is an hour not spent on the floor. Fixed installation removes a recurring labour cost and a manual-handling risk at the same time.

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | :15 · :30 · static |
| Audience | Operators, GMs, operations and WHS managers |
| Channel | Trade press, LinkedIn, hospitality operations and WHS channels. |
| Call to action | Talk to a commercial specialist. |
| Measured on | Commercial enquiry volume; rep conversations logged. |

**Production note** — Also lands as a workplace-safety argument in AUS, where manual handling is a live compliance conversation. Keep it observational, never preachy.
